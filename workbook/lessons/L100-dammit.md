# Lesson 100 — “Dammit” · Blink-182 (Tom DeLonge)

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Blink-182, with Tom DeLonge’s right hand in the spotlight
- **Song:** “Dammit”
- **Album:** *Dude Ranch* (1997)
- **The tone in one sentence:** A bright bridge-humbucker punk tone driving a four-chord loop with all-downstroke urgency and almost no decoration.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** C major; standard tuning, E A D G B E
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Tom DeLonge’s late-'90s sound is commonly remembered as simple and direct: hot bridge pickup, bright amp gain, and downstrokes. The exact *Dude Ranch* studio chain is not something I would overstate, especially because live rigs and later signature rigs get blended into the lore.

- **Guitar:** DeLonge is widely associated with Strat-style guitars using a high-output bridge humbucker. A Seymour Duncan Invader-style pickup is part of the later signature vocabulary, but the practical target is any loud bridge humbucker.
- **Amp:** Mesa/Boogie and Marshall-style high-gain sounds are commonly tied to his broader era. For this song, keep the gain punk-tight rather than metal-saturated.
- **Pedals:** The core sound does not need much beyond amp distortion. If you use a pedal, keep it bright and controlled.
- **Studio:** The guitars are dry and forward. Exact mic models and settings are not worth inventing.

This is a tone built for one job: make a simple progression feel urgent for three minutes.

## The Tone Recipe

Dial a bright crunch that keeps the low strings readable.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Bridge humbucker if possible; bridge P-90 or hot single-coil if not.
- **Amp:** Mesa-style crunch, Marshall-style crunch, or punk rhythm model.
- **Pedal:** Distortion or overdrive with gain moderate and tone slightly bright.
- **Strings/pick:** Medium pick or heavier, fresh enough strings to keep the attack clear.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge, tone **8–10**.
- Amp: Gain **5–7**, Bass **4–5**, Mids **5–6**, Treble **6–7**.
- Pedal, if needed: Drive **4–6**, Tone **5–7**, Level at unity.
- Reverb: off.
- Pick attack: all downstrokes for the main engine; relax the shoulder so the wrist can work.

If the part hurts, slow it down. Tension is not tone.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Dammit” is built on one of pop-punk’s most useful loops: **C – G – Am – F**, or I – V – vi – IV in C major. The harmony is beginner-friendly, but the job is endurance and consistency.

The intro/rhythm idea uses a **single-note outline** of the progression before the full band turns it into power chords. That is a smart arrangement move: the listener learns the chord movement as a hook before the distorted guitars arrive.

All downstrokes matter because they make the part feel square, blunt, and adolescent in the best historical sense. Alternate picking can play the notes, but it changes the posture of the song.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments for the song’s core mechanics.

**1. Four-chord outline**

```
Feel: steady eighths, ~104 BPM. Keep it even.

B|--------------------------------|
G|--------------------------------|
D|-----0-2-----0-2-----0-2-----0-2|
A|--3---------------0-------------|
E|----------3---------------1-----|
```

## The Drills

Original exercises for the pop-punk engine.

**Drill A — Downstroke Meter (~96 BPM)**  
Build even downstrokes without locking up the arm.

```
e|----------------------------------------|
B|----------------------------------------|
G|--5-5-5-5-------------------------------|
D|--5-5-5-5--5-5-5-5--7-7-7-7--3-3-3-3--|
A|--3-3-3-3--5-5-5-5--7-7-7-7--3-3-3-3--|
E|-----------3-3-3-3--5-5-5-5--1-1-1-1--|
   D D D D  D D D D  D D D D  D D D D
```

*Builds: stamina. Stop before the wrist gets tight, then restart clean.*

**Drill B — Mute-to-Open Lift (~104 BPM)**  
Practice opening the palm mute without speeding up.

```
e|--------------------------------|
B|--------------------------------|
G|--5-5-5-5--5-5-5-5-------------|
D|--5-5-5-5--5-5-5-5--3-3-3-3----|
A|--3-3-3-3--3-3-3-3--3-3-3-3----|
E|---------------------1-1-1-1----|
   PM--------open------open
```

*Builds: dynamic lift. Same chord, same tempo, different right-hand pressure.*

**Drill C — Four-Chord Hook Writing (~100 BPM)**  
Turn a chord loop into a single-note hook.

```
e|--------------------------------|
B|--------------------------------|
G|--------------------------------|
D|-----0-2-----2-0----------------|
A|--3-------2-------0--2--3-------|
E|---------------------------1----|
   C idea   G idea   Am to F
```

*Builds: writing a memorable outline from basic harmony.*

## Make It Yours

The move is not the specific four chords. It is the discipline of choosing a simple loop, writing a hook that outlines it, and then playing the full chords with one right-hand idea. Try I – V – vi – IV in another key and force yourself to write a single-note intro before the power chords. If the hook works clean, the distorted version will feel inevitable.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:15, roughly:** the single-note outline teaching you the progression.
> - **First full-band hit:** the bridge-humbucker crunch staying bright and dry.
> - **Verse rhythm:** palm muting controlling size without changing chords.
> - **Chorus sections:** all downstrokes giving the loop its blunt authority.
> - **Overall:** a beginner progression made durable by arrangement and endurance.
