# Lesson 99 — “Semi-Charmed Life” · Third Eye Blind (Kevin Cadogan)

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Third Eye Blind, with Kevin Cadogan’s bright rhythm-guitar architecture in focus
- **Song:** “Semi-Charmed Life”
- **Album:** *Third Eye Blind* (1997)
- **The tone in one sentence:** Compressed, bright crunch stabs layered into a pop rhythm track that feels sunny on top and restless underneath.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** G major; standard tuning, E A D G B E
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Kevin Cadogan’s guitar work on the first Third Eye Blind record is precise, bright, and arranged. The exact session chain for this track is not reliably public at a knob-by-knob level, so the useful target is the layered behavior.

- **Guitar:** Cadogan is often associated with Gibson-style semi-hollow and solidbody guitars, but the track can be approached with any clear electric that keeps chord stabs articulate.
- **Amp:** A medium-gain British-leaning or American crunch sound gets the point across. Too much gain erases the rhythmic edges.
- **Compression:** The rhythm guitars feel controlled and forward, so light compression or a tight amp response is useful.
- **Layers:** The record’s width likely comes from multiple guitar passes. Do not expect one track with a giant tone to do the whole job.

The part works because the guitars are bright and clipped, not because they are heavy.

## The Tone Recipe

Build a crunchy pop rhythm sound with a clean edge.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Semi-hollow, Les Paul-style, Telecaster, or Strat bridge pickup. Choose the pickup that keeps the attack clear.
- **Amp:** Vox-style crunch, Marshall-style crunch with gain low, or a modeler set to bright rhythm.
- **Pedal:** Low-to-medium overdrive, not thick distortion.
- **Double-track:** If recording, track one part slightly cleaner and one slightly crunchier.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge or bridge+middle, tone **7–10**.
- Amp: Gain **4–5**, Bass **4**, Mids **5–6**, Treble **6–7**.
- Overdrive: Drive **2–4**, Tone **6**, Level at unity.
- Compression: light, fast enough to catch hard stabs.
- Pick attack: crisp downstrokes and clipped upstrokes; mute between hits.

If the rhythm feels messy, shorten the notes before changing the sound.

## What’s Going On Musically

The song largely lives in a **G – D – C** orbit: I, V, IV in G major. On paper that is as plain as it gets. The arrangement makes it move by using syncopated stabs, partial chords, and rhythmic layering.

The guitar often behaves like a percussion section. Chords are clipped short, dead strings fill the gaps, and the accents land in places that make the vocal feel buoyant. This is not strumming around a campfire. It is pop arranging with a distorted guitar.

Because the harmony is simple, the **tone and rhythm** carry the personality. Bright crunch lets the stabs cut without turning the song into hard rock.

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the track.

**Drill A — Clip the Chord (~104 BPM)**  
Train tight note lengths on simple open chords.

```
e|--3-x-3-x-2-x-2-x-|--0-x-0-x-0-x-0-x--|
B|--3-x-3-x-3-x-3-x-|--1-x-1-x-1-x-1-x--|
G|--0-x-0-x-2-x-2-x-|--0-x-0-x-0-x-0-x--|
D|--0-x-0-x-0-x-0-x-|--2-x-2-x-2-x-2-x--|
A|--2-x-2-x---------|--3-x-3-x-3-x-3-x--|
E|--3-x-3-x---------|--------------------|
```

*Builds: fretting-hand release. The mute belongs to the groove.*

**Drill B — Pop-Rock Accent Grid (~96 BPM)**  
Keep 16ths moving while accenting only a few hits.

```
e|--x-x-3-x-x-x-2-x-|--x-x-0-x-x-x-0-x--|
B|--x-x-3-x-x-x-3-x-|--x-x-1-x-x-x-1-x--|
G|--x-x-0-x-x-x-2-x-|--x-x-0-x-x-x-0-x--|
D|------------------|--------------------|
A|------------------|--------------------|
E|------------------|--------------------|
      >       >           >       >
```

*Builds: selective accents. The hand moves constantly, but not every motion becomes a chord.*

**Drill C — Two-Layer Rhythm (~100 BPM)**  
Practice a low stab part, then a high sparkle part you could double over it.

```
Layer 1:
e|------------------|------------------|
B|------------------|------------------|
G|--0-x-0-x-2-x-2-x-|--0-x-0-x-0-x-0-x-|
D|--0-x-0-x-0-x-0-x-|--2-x-2-x-2-x-2-x-|
A|--2-x-2-x---------|--3-x-3-x-3-x-3-x-|
E|--3-x-3-x---------|------------------|

Layer 2:
e|--3---3---2---2---|--0---0---0---0---|
B|--3---3---3---3---|--1---1---1---1---|
G|------------------|------------------|
D|------------------|------------------|
A|------------------|------------------|
E|------------------|------------------|
```

*Builds: arranging with layers instead of one oversized guitar part.*

## Make It Yours

Take a three-chord progression and make it work using only rhythm. First play it as full open chords. Then cut every chord in half with the fretting hand. Then add a high-string double-track that only plays the top two strings. That is how this kind of bright crunch becomes pop arrangement rather than background strumming.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20, roughly:** bright guitar stabs creating motion before the chorus payoff.
> - **Verse groove:** muted strums and clipped chords acting like percussion.
> - **Chorus width:** layered guitars making simple harmony feel larger.
> - **Top end:** enough treble to sparkle, not enough fizz to scrape.
> - **Overall:** a sunny guitar sound with a nervous right hand underneath.
