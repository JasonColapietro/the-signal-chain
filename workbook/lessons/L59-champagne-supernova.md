# Lesson 59 — “Champagne Supernova” · Oasis

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Oasis (Noel Gallagher)
- **Song:** “Champagne Supernova”
- **Album:** *(What’s the Story) Morning Glory?* (1995)
- **Section:** THE 90s
- **The tone in one sentence:** Semi-hollow Britpop layering turns open chords into a wide, chiming bed, then lets thicker lead and rhythm overdubs lift the whole song without losing the strum.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** A major / A mixolydian color; standard tuning, E A D G B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Noel Gallagher’s mid-'90s sound is less about one exposed guitar track and more about layered parts that add up to a huge, familiar shimmer.

- **Guitar:** **Epiphone Sheraton, ES-335-style, Les Paul-style, and other semi-hollow or humbucker guitars** are commonly associated with Gallagher’s Britpop era. For this lesson, the semi-hollow idea matters because it gives open chords body and chime.
- **Amp:** Vox-style and Marshall-style British amp sounds are commonly cited around Oasis recordings and live rigs. Exact per-track blends should be treated as approximate.
- **Effects:** Light modulation, delay, and reverb can be part of the width, but the core is still strummed guitars layered with intent.
- **Layering:** The recorded sound likely depends more on multiple guitar parts than on a single magic setting. One guitar should sound good alone; several should sound like a record.

The trick is to keep each layer simple. If every overdub tries to be the main event, the song gets smaller.

## The Tone Recipe

Create one clear strum tone, then add width carefully.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Semi-hollow with humbuckers is ideal. A Les Paul works. A Tele or Strat can work if you use a warmer pickup and avoid brittle treble.
- **Amp:** Vox-style clean/crunch, Marshall-style light drive, or a modeler in either family.
- **Drive:** Low-to-medium gain overdrive for body. Do not turn the rhythm bed into hard rock crunch.
- **Effects:** Light delay or chorus can widen the part. Keep the mix low.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Gain: **3–5** for rhythm, **5–6** for lead overdubs.
- Bass: **4–6**, mids: **5–7**, treble: **5–7**.
- Pickup: neck or middle setting for warm strums; bridge for a brighter overdub.
- Reverb: **2–4**, delay mix **1–3** if used.
- Modulation: **1–3**, slow and subtle.
- Pick attack: broad, even strums, with open strings ringing clearly.

If you record layers, make the second part brighter or higher instead of simply louder. Layering works when the guitars occupy different spaces.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Champagne Supernova” lives in a broad **A-centered** world, with open chords and ringing tones giving the harmony its floating quality. The exact mood comes from simple shapes stretched across time. The song does not need dense jazz harmony; it needs chords that ring long enough to become atmosphere.

The main guitar idea is **layered open-chord architecture**. One part supplies the bed. Another part adds sparkle. A heavier or higher part can lift a later section. This is a very different discipline from playing a single riff perfectly. You are building a stack.

The semi-hollow voice helps because it has both body and air. It can strum without sounding thin, but it also leaves room for vocals and cymbals. That is the Britpop rhythm lesson: big guitar does not always mean high gain. Sometimes it means several moderate tones arranged well.

Rhythmically, keep the strum relaxed. If the hand gets too aggressive too early, the song has nowhere to grow. Let the early chords feel almost suspended, then increase density as the arrangement opens.

## Make It Yours

The transferable idea is **layering as tone**. Record or loop one open-chord part with low gain. Add a second guitar an octave higher, then a third part with slightly more drive. Keep each layer simple enough that it would feel incomplete alone. Together, they should feel wide.

This is also a good antidote to over-gain. If the guitar does not sound big, try adding a contrasting layer before adding more distortion. A semi-hollow or warm humbucker tone can fill space without flattening the song.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:00:** the opening texture feels wide before it feels heavy.
> - **Around 0:45:** open chords ring long enough to become atmosphere.
> - **Around 2:00:** additional guitars lift the section through density and brightness.
> - **Around 4:00:** the rhythm bed remains simple even as the track gets larger.
> - **Throughout:** the semi-hollow-style body keeps the strums full without excessive gain.
