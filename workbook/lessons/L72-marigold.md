# Lesson 72 — “Marigold” · Periphery (Misha Mansoor)

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Periphery (Misha Mansoor)
- **Song:** “Marigold”
- **Album:** *Periphery III: Select Difficulty* (2016)
- **Section:** THE 2010s
- **The tone in one sentence:** The modern high-gain modeler sound made playable: tight low strings, fast gates, polished cab simulation, and enough clarity for big chords to stay musical.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** Commonly taught in drop C (C G C F A D), with C-centered minor and suspended colors. If your source uses a different transcription, keep the interval shapes and the muting concept.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Misha Mansoor's importance here is not just "heavy guitar." The important shift is that a fully digital or digitally managed rig becomes the normal professional tool. By the mid-2010s, amp modelers are not demos or backups; they are the sound of the record.

- **Guitar:** Mansoor is commonly associated with extended-range and down-tuned guitars using clear, high-output pickups, including signature and Bare Knuckle-style pickup families. For this specific song, I would describe the target as a stable, intonated guitar in drop tuning rather than pretend one model is the whole story.
- **Front end:** A tight boost or overdrive before the amp model is the common djent recipe: low drive, high level, bass trimmed before distortion.
- **Amp model:** 5150/Peavey-style and modern high-gain amp models are the usual reference point for this school of tone. The exact patch is not the lesson; the behavior is.
- **Cab simulation:** Impulse responses or cab blocks are central. The low end is filtered, the upper mids are controlled, and the pick attack is placed forward.
- **Gate:** A fast gate is part of the instrument. It should clean up rests without chopping sustained chords.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** a down-tuned guitar into a tightened high-gain modeler chain: boost, gate, amp model, cab simulation, and post-EQ. The sound is aggressive, but it is not loose. The low string should hit like a drum and then get out of the way.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any well-set-up six-string that can hold drop C. Heavier strings help, but the exact gauge depends on scale length and your touch.
- **Boost:** Tube Screamer-style drive or a modeler boost with drive low and level high.
- **Amp:** Any modern high-gain model: 5150-style, Rectifier-style tightened up, or a dedicated djent/modern metal preset.
- **Cab:** Use a tight 4x12 cab sim or IR. Avoid excessive sub-bass.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Boost drive: **0–2**. Boost level: **6–8**. Tone: **5–7**.
- Amp gain: **4–6**, lower than most players expect.
- Bass: **3–5**. Mids: **5–7**. Treble/presence: **5–7**.
- Gate: **medium-fast**, just enough to stop the low string between hits.
- Picking: **firm, small motion, near the bridge**.

If the sound gets bigger but the rhythm gets less clear, reduce gain before you add anything else.

## What's Going On Musically

"Marigold" teaches the moment where **djent** becomes more than a production trick. The low-string chugs are precise, but the song is full of broad, almost orchestral chord colors: suspended voicings, add9 shapes, and high melodic tones ringing over a mechanical low end.

The basic concept is **rhythmic displacement**. The low C may hit in tight patterns that feel like drum programming, while the fretting hand grabs shapes that would collapse under a looser amp. The modeler rig lets those two jobs coexist: low-string percussion and upper-string harmony.

Theory-wise, listen for minor-key gravity with a lot of **suspended** color. A sus chord replaces the 3rd with the 2nd or 4th, which keeps the harmony open. That openness is why big distorted chords can sound wide instead of muddy.

Short fragments for study, not a full transcription.

**1. The low-string machine**

```
Feel: tight 16ths, ~100 BPM. Drop C.

   PM  PM PM    >   PM

*Caption:* The sound is in the spacing. Every palm mute needs the same size and weight.

**2. Open-color chord stab**

```

*Caption:* Suspended color over a low pedal. Too much gain will blur the upper notes.

*Caption:* The octave slide gives motion while the low C keeps the grid locked.

## The Drills

Original exercises for the style.

**Drill 1 — Low-String Gate Grid.** Target: **96 BPM**. Make every mute the same length.

```
Drop C. Count 1-e-&-a carefully.

d|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
F|----------------------------|
C|----------------------------|
G|----------------------------|
C|--0-0-0---0--0-0---0-0-0----|
   PM PM    >  PM    PM PM
```

*Builds:* right-hand consistency and gate-friendly rests. If the gate chatters, clean up the hands first.

**Drill 2 — Chord Color Over Pedal.** Target: **84 BPM**. Keep upper notes clear over low-string hits.

```
Let the top chord ring; mute only the low pedal.

d|--0---------0---------0-----|
A|--0---------0---------0-----|
F|--7---------9---------5-----|
C|--7---------9---------5-----|
G|--5---------7---------3-----|
C|--0--0-0----0--0-0----0-----|
      PM PM      PM PM
```

*Builds:* separation between chord sustain and palm-muted rhythm.

**Drill 3 — Modeler Gain Audit.** Target: **72 BPM**. Same riff, three gain ranges; keep the clearest one.

```
Play once at gain 4, once at 5, once at 6.

d|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
F|----------------------------|
C|--------5-----------7-------|
G|--------5-----------7-------|
C|--0-0-0-5--0-0-0----7-------|
   PM PM    PM PM
```

*Builds:* hearing the difference between heaviness and clarity. The best setting is usually the one where the chord still has a front edge.

## Make It Yours

The usable takeaway is simple: heavy tone is a chain of filters, not just a gain knob. Tune the guitar well, trim bass before distortion, keep the gate honest, and leave enough mids for the notes to speak. Then write riffs where the low string and upper strings have different jobs. That is where the modeler stops being a shortcut and becomes an instrument.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20:** low-string attacks that stop quickly without losing size.
> - **Verse riff areas:** the balance between mechanical rhythm and ringing upper notes.
> - **Chorus sections:** wide chords that stay clear under high gain.
> - **Transitions:** the gate and muting making the band hit as one unit.
> - **Any isolated low-C figure:** pick attack before saturation.
