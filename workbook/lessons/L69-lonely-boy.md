# Lesson 69 — “Lonely Boy” · The Black Keys

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** The Black Keys (Dan Auerbach)
- **Song:** “Lonely Boy”
- **Album:** *El Camino* (2011)
- **Section:** THE 2010s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A deliberately cheap, stacked-fuzz garage tone that turns a simple blues-rock figure into a sharp modern hook.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** E minor/blues-centered rock; standard tuning, E A D G B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Dan Auerbach's sound is a useful correction to the idea that "vintage" always means polite. His guitar tones often sound small, overloaded, and memorable on purpose.

- **Guitar:** Auerbach is commonly associated with **Harmony, Supro, Teisco, and other pawnshop/vintage-style guitars** across the Black Keys catalog. The exact guitar on every layer of this track is not something I would state as certain.
- **Fuzz/drive:** **Stacked fuzz, dirty boosts, and old amp breakup** are the behavior to chase. The sound is not a smooth overdrive; it has a ragged edge and a compressed front.
- **Amp:** **Valco/Supro-style small amps** are often cited in the broader Black Keys sound. Think mid-forward, boxy, and pushed.
- **Effects:** If there is studio help, it supports the grit rather than replacing it. The main identity is guitar, fuzz, amp, and a very direct riff.
- **Mic/room:** Exact mic choices are not reliably needed for this lesson. The practical sound is close, dry-to-medium room, and a little rude.

The lesson is not that cheap gear is automatically better. It is that a limited, colorful rig can force a part to be clear.

## The Tone Recipe

Build a controlled mess.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any single-coil, P-90, or low-output humbucker guitar. Bright pickups help the fuzz stay articulate.
- **Fuzz:** Big Muff-style, Tone Bender-style, gated fuzz, or a rough overdrive stacked into another low-gain drive.
- **Amp:** Small combo, tweed/Valco/Supro model, or any amp with mids and early breakup.
- **EQ:** Keep lows tight and mids forward. Too much bass will make the riff lose its bounce.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge or bridge-plus-neck.
- Fuzz 1: Gain 4–6, Tone 5–7, Level 5–7.
- Fuzz/drive 2: Gain 2–4, Tone 4–6, Level to taste, if stacking.
- Amp: Gain 4–6, Bass 3–5, Mids 6–8, Treble 5–7.
- Reverb: 1–3, or none if the riff gets cloudy.
- Pick attack: hard but bouncy. Let the riff dance, not drag.

If the sound turns into a flat wall, lower one gain stage. Stacked fuzz works best when each layer still leaves some shape.

## What's Going On Musically

"Lonely Boy" sits in an **E blues-rock** world. The riff vocabulary is familiar: minor pentatonic, flat-third grit, and short repeated cells. What makes it modern is the compression, the production, and the way the riff is trimmed to its most useful shape.

The main idea depends on **call-and-response phrasing**. A short low figure answers itself, then the band locks into the groove. The guitar is not playing a long blues solo; it is turning blues material into a pop hook.

Rhythmically, the feel is straight but not stiff. The riff needs a little bounce in the right hand, especially when moving between low notes and higher stabs.

The tone and part are linked. A cleaner tone would make the riff feel almost too plain. The fuzz adds teeth, but the simplicity keeps the fuzz readable.

*Caption: Chords should bark and stop. If they smear, lower gain or bass.*

```
   minor pentatonic snap
```

*Caption: A small bend into the low E gives the line its blues accent without turning it into a long solo.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — fuzz rhythm, short hooks, and garage control.

**Drill 1 — Two-Gain-Stage Riff Test**

```
~132 BPM. Play once with one fuzz, once with two.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|--------2--0--------2--------|
A|--2--2--------2--0-----------|
E|--0--0-----------------------|
```

*Builds: hearing when stacked gain helps and when it hides the riff. Keep whichever setting preserves the rhythm best.*

**Drill 2 — Boxy Amp Chord Punch**

```
~120 BPM. Hit, mute, breathe.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--9-x-7-x-9-x-12-x----------|
D|--9-x-7-x-9-x-12-x----------|
A|--7-x-5-x-7-x-10-x----------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: making fuzz chords short and rhythmic. Tone: mids high, bass controlled, reverb low.*

**Drill 3 — E Minor Hook Economy**

```
~150 BPM. No extra notes.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|--------2--0-----------------|
A|--2--0--------2--------------|
E|---------------0--3--0-------|
```

*Builds: writing a concise blues-rock hook. Repeat it until the timing feels better than adding a fill.*

## Make It Yours

The Black Keys lesson is that fuzz can be a songwriting tool if the part is simple enough to survive it. Write a two-bar E minor riff with no more than five notes. Then try it through one fuzz, then two, then a small amp voice. Keep the version where the hook gets clearer, not merely louder.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **direct fuzz riff** arriving without a polished intro.
> - Around 0:18, the guitar tone staying ragged but still readable.
> - Around 0:55, the riff functioning as a pop hook rather than a blues jam.
> - Around 1:50, stacked grit giving the track shape without burying the drums.
> - Around 2:55, the final repetitions holding attention through tone and rhythm.
