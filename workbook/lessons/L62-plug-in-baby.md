# Lesson 62 — “Plug In Baby” · Muse

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Muse (Matt Bellamy)
- **Song:** “Plug In Baby”
- **Album:** *Origin of Symmetry* (2001)
- **Section:** THE 2000s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A bright, compressed, slightly unstable fuzz lead that turns classical-style motion into a modern rock siren.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** B minor; standard tuning, E A D G B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Matt Bellamy’s early-2000s tone is where the guitar starts to feel like a controller as much as a traditional instrument. The broad strokes are well known; the exact session chain should be treated carefully.

- **Guitar:** A **Manson-style guitar** with a bridge humbucker is the tool most associated with Bellamy in this era. The famous story is the **Z.Vex Fuzz Factory-style circuit** built into the guitar, which is commonly cited as part of his gated, sputtering lead sound.
- **Fuzz:** A **Fuzz Factory** or similar gated fuzz is the important behavior: compressed attack, bright harmonic spray, and a tendency to misbehave if the stability/gate controls are pushed.
- **Amp:** Bellamy is commonly associated with high-headroom British gain platforms and blended live rigs, but for this lesson the practical target is a bright amp that stays articulate under fuzz.
- **Other effects:** Whammy, delay, and modulation appear across Bellamy’s wider vocabulary, but the riff here lives mostly on fuzz, precision, and the pickup.
- **Mic/room:** Exact recording details for the guitar track are not something I would state as fact. Treat detailed mic recipes as lore unless the source is first-hand.

The point is not just “more fuzz.” It is fuzz disciplined by clean fretting and even picking.

## The Tone Recipe

This sound is easier to approximate than to control.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker guitar, especially a bright bridge pickup. A P-90 guitar can work if it stays clear.
- **Fuzz:** A Fuzz Factory-style pedal is closest. A gated fuzz, octave fuzz with the octave low in the mix, or a digital model with gate and bias controls can also work.
- **Amp:** A clean-to-crunch British model, or any clear amp with the treble not too soft.
- **Noise control:** A light gate can help, but do not clamp the note tails so hard that the riff loses sustain.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge humbucker.
- Fuzz: Gain/Fuzz 6–8, Gate 4–7, Compression/Bias in the middle-to-high range if your pedal has it, Output to match the amp.
- Amp: Gain 3–5, Bass 3–5, Mids 5–7, Treble 6–7.
- Delay/Reverb: very light, if any, because fast notes get messy quickly.
- Pick attack: even alternate picking. The fuzz should not be asked to hide sloppy timing.

If the line splatters apart, lower the fuzz or gate before blaming your fingers. The best Bellamy-style fuzz still has a clear note in the middle of the noise.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Plug In Baby” is centered on **B minor**, and the opening figure borrows the drama of classical sequence writing: a line climbs, turns, and keeps re-aiming itself until the band drops in.

The main idea is built from **minor-scale motion** with strong chromatic color. It sounds almost baroque because the notes are packed close together and the contour keeps moving. On guitar, that means two jobs at once: fretting hand accuracy and picking hand evenness. Fuzz exaggerates both.

The riff also teaches **position shifting**. You cannot keep everything under one comfortable box without making the line awkward, so you slide and cross strings cleanly. The trick is to hear the phrase as one melody, not a pile of finger moves.

Harmonically, the song is less about a jazz-style chord map and more about a rock band using a lead riff as the identity of the track. The bass and guitar lock around the minor center while the vocal sits above it.

*Caption: The riff’s drama comes from close notes under a very bright fuzz. Fret cleanly or the circuit will smear the melody.*

*Caption: This is the Bellamy problem in miniature: classical contour, rock attack, and a fuzz tone that magnifies every uneven note.*

```
   B5       D5     C#5
```

*Caption: Let the gate chop the silence, but make the mutes with your hands first.*

## The Drills

These are original exercises in the style of the song — precision first, fuzz second.

**Drill 1 — Gated Fuzz Scale Ladder**

```
~120 BPM. Alternate pick every note.

e|----------------------7-9-10-|
B|---------------7-8-10--------|
G|--------6-7-9----------------|
D|--7-9-10---------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: clean minor-scale shifting under a compressed fuzz. Tone: bridge pickup, fuzz just gated enough to keep rests quiet.*

**Drill 2 — Chromatic Turnaround Builder**

```
~112 BPM. Four-note cells, perfectly even.

e|-----------------------------|
B|--8-7-6---6-7-8---10-9-8-----|
G|--------7-------7--------9---|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: the close-step tension that makes the riff feel nervous. Keep every fret close to the wire and avoid pulling the notes sharp.*

**Drill 3 — Fuzz Stab Discipline**

```
~136 BPM. Downstrokes on chords, hard mutes between.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--4--x--4--x--6--x--7--x-----|
D|--4--x--4--x--6--x--7--x-----|
A|--2--x--2--x--4--x--5--x-----|
E|-----------------------------|
   >     >     >     >
```

*Builds: controlling the silence around high-gain, gated chords. The rests should sound intentional, not like the pedal closing by accident.*

## Make It Yours

The reusable lesson is that an effect can become part of the composition. A Fuzz Factory-style sound is not a polite overdrive; it pushes you toward short phrases, fast ornaments, stabs, and silence. Write a minor-scale line, then turn the fuzz up until the line almost falls apart. Back it down just enough that the melody returns. That edge is where this kind of 2000s guitar lives.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **bright fuzz lead** staying clear even as the notes move quickly.
> - Around 0:14, the band entering under a riff that already defined the track.
> - Around 0:45, the way the guitar **switches from melody to stabs** without changing personality.
> - Around 1:35, the fuzz compression making single notes feel almost synthetic.
> - Around 2:45, the final energy coming from repetition and control rather than extra gain.
