# Lesson 63 — “Schism” · Tool

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Tool (Adam Jones)
- **Song:** “Schism”
- **Album:** *Lateralus* (2001)
- **Section:** THE 2000s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A dark Les Paul voice into a blended wall of amps, used with surprising restraint over drop-D odd-meter tension.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** D-centered minor/modal rock; drop D tuning, D A D G B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Adam Jones's sound is heavy, but it is not a scooped modern metal wash. It has a hard midrange edge and a sculpted top that lets small rhythmic parts read clearly.

- **Guitar:** A **Gibson Les Paul Custom**, with the Silverburst model commonly cited as the guitar most associated with Jones, is the center of the story. A bridge humbucker gives the tight low end and focused attack.
- **Amps:** Jones is commonly associated with a **blended amp setup**, with Diezel, Marshall, Mesa/Boogie, and other high-gain platforms cited in different eras. The exact album blend should be treated as approximate; the behavior is a layered wall with definition.
- **Effects:** Delay, flanger, and other modulation appear across his vocabulary, but "Schism" teaches how little you need when the riff and meter are strong.
- **Gain:** Moderate-to-high, but not fizzy. The low D needs to stay percussive.
- **Mic/room:** The exact cabinet and mic details are not something I would invent. The usable lesson is tight close-mic weight plus enough room/mix space to let the riff breathe.

The important part is discipline. Jones does not fill every second. The guitar often plays like a machine part inside a larger rhythmic design.

## The Tone Recipe

You can approximate the feel without owning the famous rig.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker guitar. A Les Paul-style instrument is closest, but an SG, PRS-style, or superstrat bridge humbucker works.
- **Amp:** A high-gain British or modern amp model with strong mids. Avoid an extreme scoop.
- **Pedals:** A light overdrive boost can tighten the low end if your amp gets loose. Keep modulation off until the core rhythm is stable.
- **Tuning:** Drop D is required for the one-finger low-string shapes and the weight of the riff language.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge humbucker.
- Amp: Gain 5–7, Bass 4–6, Mids 6–8, Treble 4–6, Presence 3–5.
- Boost: optional, low drive with output in the middle-to-high range if the amp needs tightening.
- Reverb/Delay: very low, if any, for the main riff work.
- Pick attack: firm downstrokes for low notes, lighter touch for upper-string answers.

If the low D blooms too much, reduce bass before reducing gain. The riff needs weight, but it also needs edges.

## What's Going On Musically

"Schism" is famous for its **uneven phrasing**. Rather than sitting in a comfortable four-bar grid, the parts feel like they are connecting and disconnecting, which is exactly why the title fits the music.

The guitar is in **drop D**, so the low three strings can form power-chord shapes with one finger. That tuning gives you two things: heavy low roots and fast movement between low-string stabs and higher melodic fragments.

Rhythmically, the song is often discussed in shifting odd groupings. The practical way to learn the feel is not to argue over every barline at first. Count the repeated cell out loud, feel where it turns around, and let the bass and drums teach you where the downbeat returns.

Melodically, the guitar leans on **minor seconds, fourths, and fifths** rather than blues bends. The mood comes from spacing and interval choice: dark, patient, architectural.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments below — characteristic drop-D tools rather than a full transcription.

**1. Drop-D low-string restraint**

```
Tuning: D A D G B E. Feel: controlled eighths, ~108 BPM.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|--0-----0-----3-----2--------|
A|--0-----0-----3-----2--------|
D|--0--0--0--0--3--0--2--0-----|
      PM    PM       PM
```

*Caption: The low string supplies weight, but the rests give it shape. Keep the palm mute tight and even.*

**2. Uneven-cell riffing**

```
Count: 5 + 7. Let the pattern turn around naturally.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--------5-----------5--------|
D|--0--3-----3--0--0-----3--2--|
A|--0-----------0--0-----------|
D|--0-----------0--0-----------|
   1  2  3  4  5  1  2  3...
```

*Caption: Odd meter feels less mysterious when you treat it as a repeated phrase length instead of a math problem.*

**3. High-register answer**

```
Feel: quieter, slightly behind the low riff.

e|-----------------------------|
B|--6~-----5-------------------|
G|------------7--5~------------|
D|--------------------7--------|
A|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
   small upper voice over the machine
```

*Caption: The upper phrase gives the riff a human answer. Use less gain in your hands, not a new patch.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — drop-D weight, odd grouping, and restraint.

**Drill 1 — Drop-D Weight Control**

```
~96 BPM. Palm-mute only the marked low notes.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|--0-----0-----5-----3--------|
A|--0-----0-----5-----3--------|
D|--0--0--0--0--5--0--3--0-----|
      PM    PM       PM
```

*Builds: muting consistency on the low D while letting chord hits speak. Tone: bridge humbucker, mids forward, bass controlled.*

**Drill 2 — Five-Plus-Seven Loop**

```
~104 BPM. Count 1-2-3-4-5, then 1-2-3-4-5-6-7.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--------4--------------4-----|
D|--0--2-----2--0--0--2-----2--|
A|--0-----------0--0-----------|
D|--0-----------0--0-----------|
   five notes       seven-note feel
```

*Builds: keeping an uneven phrase calm. Do not speed up at the turnaround.*

**Drill 3 — Heavy-to-Quiet Answer**

```
~88 BPM. Bar 1 heavy, bar 2 quiet.

e|----------------|----------------|
B|----------------|--6~---5--------|
G|----------------|---------7--5~--|
D|--0--0--3--2----|----------------|
A|--0--0--3--2----|----------------|
D|--0--0--3--2----|----------------|
   low machine      upper answer
```

*Builds: dynamic control without changing channels. Pick the upper answer softer and let it sit inside the same amp sound.*

## Make It Yours

The "Schism" lesson is that heaviness does not require constant motion. Put your guitar in drop D, choose a five- or seven-note cell, and repeat it until the pattern becomes physical. Then remove one note. Add one rest. Move the answer up an octave. You will find that the most interesting heavy riffs often come from subtraction and placement, not from more gain.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **interlocking rhythm** before the full guitar weight arrives.
> - Around 0:45, the guitar staying **dry and defined** instead of washing over the bass.
> - Around 1:20, the way the riff turns through uneven groupings without sounding random.
> - Around 3:05, heavier sections still keeping the low D tight.
> - Around 5:40, the final build using repetition and restraint as much as distortion.
