- **Album:** *St. Vincent* (2014)
- **Section:** THE 2010s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A dry, angular guitar voice where fuzz, gating, and surgical muting turn the riff into something closer to a sequencer than a blues-rock roar.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Annie Clark's guitar sound in this era is a good 2010s lesson because the rig stops behaving like a vintage chain and starts behaving like a designed system. The guitar is still in your hands, but the edges are being squared off by fuzz, compression, filtering, and hard stops.

- **Guitar:** Clark is commonly associated with bright, compact solidbody guitars, including her Harmony and Ernie Ball Music Man-era instruments. The exact guitar used for this track is not published at the level where I would call it settled, so the honest target is a clear, immediate guitar with enough upper-mid bite to survive heavy processing.
- **Gain:** A gated fuzz or tight distortion is the tool most associated with this kind of tone. The important behavior is not just dirt; it is the way the sound shuts off quickly when the fretting hand mutes.
- **Control:** Noise gates, abrupt muting, and right-hand precision matter as much as the pedal. If the gate is doing all the work, the part gets stiff. If the hands are doing none of it, the riff smears.
- **Amp or modeler:** The recorded chain is not reliably documented in enough detail to name a single amp. A clean-to-firm amp platform or a modeler with a tight, bright amp voice gets the behavior right.
- **Studio detail:** Exact mic choices, gate settings, and fuzz models are not solid public information, so I would not build the lesson around a secret setting.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** a bright guitar into a clipped, gated gain stage, then into a fairly controlled amp or modeler. The sound should feel narrow, percussive, and almost dry. Sustain is available when she lets a note hang, but the default gesture is chopped.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any Strat, Tele, offset, or bright humbucker guitar. Use the bridge pickup or a split-coil setting if you have one.
- **Gain:** A fuzz with a bias/gate control, a square-edged distortion, or a modeler fuzz block. A Big Muff-style pedal can work if you tighten the bass and keep the notes short.
- **Gate:** Use a noise gate lightly, just enough that rests go black. Do not clamp so hard that pick attack disappears.
- **Amp:** Clean to edge-of-breakup, with the bass lower than you think and mids present.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Gain/fuzz: **5–7**, with the fuzz texture more important than volume.
- Gate threshold: **low-to-medium**, set so muted stops cut off but sustained notes still speak.
- Amp bass: **3–5**. Mids: **5–7**. Treble: **5–7**.
- Guitar tone: **7–10**, depending on how sharp your bridge pickup is.
- Pick attack: **short, close to the bridge, and very even**.

The sound should not bloom like classic rock fuzz. It should appear, make its point, and disappear.

## What's Going On Musically

The part sits in an E-centered rock world, but the hook is less about a long melody than about **rhythmic geometry**. Short notes, rests, and small chromatic moves do the work. That is why the tone matters: a smeared rig turns the riff into mush, while a gated one makes the rests part of the groove.

The vocabulary is familiar enough — minor pentatonic shapes, chromatic approach notes, octave jumps, and clipped power-chord fragments — but the attitude is different from a blues-rock line. The guitar does not lean back and sing. It jabs, stops, and re-enters at odd angles.

The 2010s lesson here is that guitar tone can be **edited at the source**. You are not just choosing clean or dirty. You are shaping note length, envelope, and negative space.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments only — enough to learn the gesture without turning this into a full transcription.

**1. The gated single-note cell**

```
Feel: tight 16ths, ~96 BPM. Kill every rest cleanly.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|----------------------------|
D|--------2-----2-------------|
A|--2--4-----4-----2--x--2----|
E|--0--0-----0-----0--x--0----|
   >  >     >     >     >
```

*Caption:* The open low E is not a blanket. It is a switch. Let it sound, then cut it off.

**2. Octave snap**

```
Feel: clipped and dry, ~96 BPM.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|--9--x--11--x--12--11-------|
D|--x--x--x---x--x---x--------|
A|--7--x--9---x--10--9--------|
E|----------------------------|
   E     F#     G   F#
```

*Caption:* Octaves give the line a synthetic edge without needing more gain. The dead strokes keep it from floating.

**3. Bent fuzz answer**

```
Feel: brief vocal bend, then immediate mute.

e|----------------------------|
B|--12b14r12--10--------------|
G|---------------11--9--x-----|
D|---------------------x------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
   let it squeal    shut door
```

*Caption:* One expressive bend is enough. The mute after it is part of the phrase.

## The Drills

Original exercises for the tone and technique.

**Drill 1 — Gate the Rest.** Target: **92 BPM**. Train the left hand to create silence before the pedal does.

```
Straight 16ths. Fuzz on, gate light.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|----------------------------|
D|--------2-----------2-------|
A|--2--4-----x--2--4-----x----|
E|--0--0-----x--0--0-----x----|
   >  >        >  >
```

*Builds:* clipped note endings and even accents. If the muted x notes ring, slow down.

**Drill 2 — Angular Octave Ladder.** Target: **104 BPM**. Move octave shapes without losing the dry attack.

```
Keep both hands close to the strings.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|--9--11--12--11--9--7-------|
D|--x--x---x---x---x--x-------|
A|--7--9---10--9---7--5-------|
E|----------------------------|
   E  F#  G   F#  E  D
```

*Builds:* position shifts and muting across skipped strings. Let the shapes pop, not ring together.

**Drill 3 — Fuzz Bend, Hard Stop.** Target: **84 BPM**. Practice one sustained gesture followed by a clean cutoff.

```
Count the rest as carefully as the bend.

e|----------------------------|
B|--10b12~~~--10--x-----------|
G|----------------x--9--7-----|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
   bend hold  stop answer
```

*Builds:* controlled sustain inside a mostly percussive sound. The mute should feel intentional, not accidental.

## Make It Yours

Take any riff you already know and remove half the sustain. Keep the notes, but shorten them until the rests start carrying the groove. Then add a gated fuzz or tight distortion and see which parts survive. If a phrase still works when it is dry, clipped, and exposed, it is probably strong. That is the deeper lesson here: effects can make guitar larger, but they can also make it more exact.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:15:** the guitar's clipped entrance — the rests are as audible as the notes.
> - **0:20–0:40:** the way fuzz stays narrow instead of spreading across the whole mix.
> - **Around the vocal entrances:** guitar figures answer in short shapes rather than long fills.
> - **Chorus sections:** thicker texture, but still controlled note length.
> - **Any exposed riff return:** the gate-like cutoff after the attack.
