# Lesson 107 — “Feel Good Drag” · Anberlin

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Anberlin
- **Song:** “Feel Good Drag”
- **Album:** *New Surrender* (2008)
- **The tone in one sentence:** A drop-tuned modern-rock surge, with tight low strings, sharp octave hooks, and enough midrange to keep the riff from turning into mud.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** Drop D for this lesson, D A D G B E; minor-key riff language centered around the low D string.
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

The Anberlin guitar sound on “Feel Good Drag” is polished but still physical. Exact studio rigs are not usually documented in public detail, so the safe description is behavioral: humbuckers, drop tuning, controlled high gain, and layered guitars that hit hard without losing pick definition.

- **Guitars:** The band is commonly associated with **humbucker-equipped rock guitars** in live and studio contexts. The exact instrument on the recording is not something to state with false certainty. Use a bridge humbucker if you have one.
- **Amps:** A **Mesa, Marshall, Orange, or similar modern rock amp** is the right family of sound. You want tight lows and present mids, not scooped metal fizz.
- **Pedals:** A boost or overdrive in front of the amp is a common way to tighten drop-tuned guitars, but it should not add much extra gain. Noise gates are useful if they do not chop off the riff’s sustain.
- **Layers:** Rhythm guitars are likely doubled or layered. The recorded weight comes from tight performances more than one absurdly gained track.

Drop D makes power shapes easy, but it also exposes sloppy muting. The lower string will ring whether you invited it or not.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** tight drop-tuned crunch, layered rhythm guitars, and a cutting lead/octave presence. The exact chain is not reliably published, so dial for attack and separation.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Bridge humbucker. If you have single-coils, reduce treble and keep the gain moderate.
- **Amp/model:** Modern rock or British high-gain channel.
- **Boost:** Tube Screamer-style drive with drive low, level moderate, tone to taste.
- **Noise control:** Gate lightly if needed, but keep sustain natural.

**Starting ranges (out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **5-7**.
- Bass: **4-5**, mids: **6-7**, treble: **5-6**, presence **4-6**.
- Boost drive: **1-2**, level **5-7**.
- Palm mute: firm, just behind the bridge pickup area.
- Pick attack: hard downstrokes on the low string; lighter touch on octave hooks.

The tone should feel like a clenched fist, not a blanket.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Feel Good Drag” uses the simplest gift of Drop D: one-finger power chords and a low open-string pedal. That lets the guitar jump between riff notes and full chords quickly while keeping the bottom end aggressive.

The harmony is minor-leaning and riff-driven. Instead of a delicate chord progression, the song gets its identity from:

- **Low-string pedal tones:** repeated low D or low-position notes that keep tension under the riff.
- **Octave movement:** higher hooks that cut through the dense guitars.
- **Syncopation:** accents that hit around the beat rather than politely on every downbeat.
- **Release:** palm-muted pressure opening into full chords.

The low tuning does not make the part heavy by itself. The heaviness is in the muting, timing, and how cleanly the band releases into the open hits.

## The Drills

These are original exercises for tight Drop D rhythm.

**Drill 1 — Low-String Gatekeeper.** Trains muting and downstroke consistency.

```
BPM target: 132, then 154. Keep every low note the same length.

e|------------------------------------------|
B|------------------------------------------|
G|------------------------------------------|
D|--0--0--3--0--0--5--0--0--7--5--3--------|
A|--0--0--3--0--0--5--0--0--7--5--3--------|
D|--0--0--3--0--0--5--0--0--7--5--3--------|
   PM PM    PM PM    PM PM
```

*Builds: tight low-string control. If the open D blooms after each hit, move the palm slightly closer to the bridge.*

**Drill 2 — Octave Over Drop D.** Trains lead clarity above a heavy tuning.

```
BPM target: 144. Keep the muted string dead.

e|------------------------------------------|
B|------------------------------------------|
G|--7--9--10--9--7--5--7--9----------------|
D|--x--x---x--x--x--x--x--x----------------|
A|--5--7---8--7--5--3--5--7----------------|
D|------------------------------------------|
```

*Builds: octave precision. Use less gain than the rhythm patch if the notes smear.*

**Drill 3 — Pressure and Release.** Trains the verse-to-chorus move that makes the riff feel large.

```
BPM target: 154. Two beats muted, two beats open.

e|------------------------------------------|
B|------------------------------------------|
G|------------------------------------------|
D|--0--0--0--0--5--5--8--8-----------------|
A|--0--0--0--0--5--5--8--8-----------------|
D|--0--0--0--0--5--5--8--8-----------------|
   PM PM PM PM  open open open open
```

*Builds: dynamic contrast. The tempo stays locked when the mute comes off.*

## Make It Yours

Drop D is useful because it lets you simplify the left hand and focus on the right hand. Write a riff with only three fretted shapes, then make it interesting with muting, rests, and one octave answer. Keep the gain just low enough that you can hear the pick hit the string. If a riff only sounds heavy with the gain maxed, the part probably needs better rhythm. The real lesson from “Feel Good Drag” is controlled release: clamp down, build pressure, then open the chord at exactly the right moment.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00-0:15:** the low-tuned guitar staying tight instead of flubby.
> - **Verse sections:** palm-muted pressure creating motion under the vocal.
> - **Pre-chorus lift:** octave or higher-register movement cutting through the rhythm bed.
> - **Chorus sections:** open chords sounding larger because the earlier riff was restrained.
> - **Final pass:** doubled guitars staying locked; the heaviness comes from timing.
