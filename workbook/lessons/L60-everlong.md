# Lesson 60 — “Everlong” · Foo Fighters

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Foo Fighters (Dave Grohl)
- **Song:** “Everlong”
- **Album:** *The Colour and the Shape* (1997)
- **Section:** THE 90s
- **The tone in one sentence:** Drop-D chord shapes ring with chiming suspended notes, then bloom into a roaring full-band guitar wall without losing the pulse.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** Drop D, with the whole guitar commonly tuned down a half-step for the record feel: Db Ab Db Gb Bb Eb; this lesson is written in drop D shapes

## The Rig & Signal Chain

“Everlong” is one of the great 1990s rhythm-guitar parts because the tone is inseparable from the tuning and right hand.

- **Guitar:** Humbucker-equipped Gibson-style guitars, including semi-hollow models, are commonly associated with Dave Grohl’s Foo Fighters era. The practical target is a guitar that gives body to high ringing notes and authority to low drop-D shapes.
- **Amp:** Mesa/Boogie and Marshall-style high-gain rock sounds are commonly cited around this period. Exact track-by-track amp blends are not reliably documented at player-useful knob detail.
- **Tuning:** Drop D is essential to the shape of the riff. The record is commonly played a half-step below standard pitch, but practicing in plain drop D teaches the mechanics.
- **Layering:** The recorded guitar wall likely comes from layered rhythm tracks rather than one enormous setting. Keep the part tight before making it huge.

The sound should chime and roar at the same time. If it only roars, the suspended top notes disappear. If it only chimes, the chorus does not lift.

## The Tone Recipe

Tune first, then dial the amp around the riff.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker guitar. A P-90 guitar can work. Single-coils need more body from the amp or drive.
- **Amp:** High-gain British or American rock model, or a distortion pedal into a clean platform.
- **Drive:** Medium-high gain, with enough clarity for ringing upper strings.
- **Double-tracking substitute:** If playing alone, use slightly more mids and less reverb. If recording, use less gain per track and layer two tight takes.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Gain: **5–7**, not maxed.
- Bass: **4–6**, mids: **6–8**, treble: **5–7**.
- Presence: **4–6**, adjusted for pick noise.
- Guitar volume: **10**, tone **7–10**.
- Reverb: **0–2**. The part gets width from performance and layers.
- Pick attack: constant eighth-note motion, wrist loose, accents controlled.

If the low string overwhelms the top notes, lower bass and gain before raising treble. Clarity is the sound.

## What’s Going On Musically

The song’s guitar identity comes from **drop-D suspended chord shapes**. Drop D lets one finger hold big low-root power shapes while the upper strings ring with color tones. That combination creates the chime: a low foundation with high notes that do not resolve immediately.

The riff is rhythmically relentless. The right hand keeps moving, while the fretting hand changes shapes around sustained open or repeated upper notes. That gives the part its feeling of motion even when the harmony is not complicated.

The theory idea is **suspension under distortion**. A suspended chord replaces or delays the third with a note like the second or fourth. Under gain, that missing third keeps the chord emotionally open. It can feel hopeful, anxious, or unresolved depending on the arrangement.

The arrangement blooms by adding density. The intro pattern is intimate because the pulse is exposed. When the band opens up, the same kind of guitar vocabulary becomes enormous. Your job is to keep the tempo and note clarity steady across both sizes.

## Make It Yours

The main idea is **suspended motion over a low drone**. Tune to drop D, hold the low strings steady, and move a top note through two or three positions. Let the high note create the emotion while the low strings keep the engine running.

For production, remember that huge rhythm guitars are often less distorted than they seem. Record two tight takes with moderate gain before you reach for more saturation. For playing alone, keep the mids strong and the reverb low so the chime and roar stay connected.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:00:** the riff has chime because the upper notes stay clear over the low drone.
> - **Around 0:40:** the band gets larger without the guitar pulse changing shape.
> - **Around 1:15:** drop-D low strings give the rhythm part weight and speed.
> - **Around 2:35:** the guitar wall blooms through density more than extra gain.
> - **Throughout:** suspended chord colors keep the harmony emotionally open.
