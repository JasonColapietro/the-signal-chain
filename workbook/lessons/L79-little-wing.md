# Lesson 79 — “Little Wing” · Jimi Hendrix

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Jimi Hendrix
- **Song:** “Little Wing”
- **Album:** *Axis: Bold as Love* (1967)
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES
- **The tone in one sentence:** A glassy Strat rhythm-lead shimmer where chord fragments, double-stops, and a swirling studio texture turn accompaniment into melody.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** E minor shapes; Hendrix commonly tuned a half-step down, so detune to Eb standard if you want to sit with the record.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Hendrix is already in this workbook in Lessons 12 and 14; come back here after "Purple Haze" if you want to hear how the same broad vocabulary becomes tender instead of explosive.

- **Guitar:** A Fender Stratocaster is the tool most associated with Hendrix here. The neck or neck-middle area of the guitar is the practical target for the round, glassy attack.
- **Amp:** Marshall amplification is commonly tied to Hendrix's late-1960s sound, but exact settings for this track should be treated as approximate.
- **Texture:** A rotating-speaker or Leslie-style treatment is commonly cited for the shimmer on the recording. The exact routing is not something I would present as a knob-level fact.
- **Gain:** Light breakup, not fuzz-forward. The notes need to stay clear because the part is full of small chord tones.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** Strat-style guitar, neck pickup area, light amp breakup, and a gentle swirl. The chord should sparkle, but the attack should be soft enough that embellishments feel sung.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Strat neck pickup is ideal. A Tele neck pickup, P-90, or split humbucker can work if you soften the attack.
- **Amp:** Clean-to-edge amp or modeler, with a little compression from volume.
- **Modulation:** Slow rotary, vibe, or subtle chorus. Keep it slow and shallow.
- **Drive:** Low overdrive if your amp is too clean.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **2–4**.
- Bass: **4–5**. Mids: **5–7**. Treble: **5–6**.
- Rotary/vibe depth: **2–4**. Rate: **slow**.
- Guitar tone: **6–8**.
- Pick attack: **soft, often with thumb/finger support**.

The part should never feel like a pedal demo. The swirl is a halo around the chord work.

## What's Going On Musically

"Little Wing" is one of the great **rhythm-lead** studies. Hendrix does not separate chord playing from lead playing. He holds a chord shape, decorates it with hammer-ons and slides, then lets a double-stop or bass note imply the next change.

The progression moves through E minor colors, but the important concept is **chord embellishment**. Instead of strumming Em and waiting, you add the 2nd, 4th, or 6th around the grip. Those small neighboring notes make the chord breathe.

The feel is relaxed but not vague. Every embellishment has time. If you rush, the part turns into a lick collection instead of a song.

## The Drills

Original exercises for Hendrix-style rhythm-lead playing.

**Drill 1 — Chord Grip Embellishment.** Target: **64 BPM**. Keep the chord ringing while fingers move.

```
Do not lift the whole shape for each hammer-on.

e|--0----------0--------------|
B|--0h3p0------0--------------|
G|--0-----0h2--0--------------|
D|--2----------2--------------|
A|--2----------2--------------|
E|--0----------0--------------|
```

*Builds:* independence inside a chord grip. Let the open strings help.

**Drill 2 — Sliding Thirds With Space.** Target: **72 BPM**. Slide pairs, then wait.

```
Count the rest after each slide.

e|----------------|----------------|
B|--3/5--5--------|--5\3--0--------|
G|--4/6--6--------|--6\4--0--------|
D|----------------|----------------|
A|----------------|----------------|
E|----------------|----------------|
```

*Builds:* double-stop intonation and patience.

**Drill 3 — Bass-Top Conversation.** Target: **60 BPM**. Thumb or pick the bass, then answer high.

```
Keep bass warm and top notes light.

e|------3----------0----------|
B|------3------0h3---3p0------|
G|------0----------------0----|
D|----------------------------|
A|--2-------------------------|
E|--0-------------------------|
```

*Builds:* alternating low support and high melody without breaking time.

## Make It Yours

Choose any open-position chord and add only neighboring notes. Hammer on the 2nd, slide into a third, or pick a bass note before the chord. The point is not to memorize Hendrix grips. It is to stop thinking of rhythm and lead as separate jobs. Once a chord can answer itself, your playing gets more human immediately.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20:** chord fragments moving like a melody.
> - **Early fills:** double-stops sliding in and out of the harmony.
> - **Swirling texture:** modulation supporting the guitar rather than covering it.
> - **Bass-note moments:** low notes introducing the next chord color.
> - **Final phrases:** restraint and touch doing more work than gain.
