# Lesson 75 — “Are You Looking Up” · Mk.gee

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Mk.gee
- **Song:** “Are You Looking Up”
- **Album:** *Two Star & The Dream Police* (2024)
- **Section:** NOW
- **The tone in one sentence:** Guitar processed until it argues with synths: bent, filtered, compressed, and slightly unstable, but still played with human touch.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** This lesson uses E-centered standard-tuning shapes. The record’s processing makes exact pitch-and-source claims risky, so focus on the phrasing, bends, and envelope.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Mk.gee is a useful NOW lesson because the guitar does not announce itself in the old way. It is not “clean guitar plus effect.” It is a sound-design object that still responds to fingers.

- **Guitar:** Mk.gee is commonly associated with guitar setups that feed synth-like processing, including Roland-style guitar-synth and pitch/filter approaches in the broader live and studio vocabulary. I would not claim a single guitar or box as the verified source of this exact track without better documentation.
- **Processing:** The sound points toward pitch movement, compression, filtering, chorus-like spread, and preamp coloration. The important behavior is a note that bends and blooms like a synth while retaining pick/finger timing.
- **Amp:** A traditional amp may be present somewhere in the chain, but the finished tone is more about processing than amp identity.
- **Recording texture:** Slight instability is part of the charm: not lo-fi for its own sake, but enough wobble and saturation to keep the guitar from sounding polished flat.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** a guitar signal into compression, pitch/filter movement, chorus or widening, and a warm preamp or saturation stage. The tone should have a soft front edge and a strange aftertaste.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any electric. Neck or middle pickup helps if the bridge is too sharp.
- **Compression:** Medium-to-heavy compression for sustain and level.
- **Pitch/filter:** Use a subtle pitch vibrato, envelope filter, auto-wah set mildly, or a guitar-synth patch with dry signal blended in.
- **Modulation:** Chorus, vibrato, or a warbly tape-style effect.
- **Amp:** Clean or just breaking up, with rolled-off treble.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Compression: **5–7**.
- Modulation depth: **2–4**. Rate: **slow-to-medium**.
- Filter/envelope amount: **2–5**, depending on the pedal.
- Saturation/drive: **2–4**.
- Amp treble: **3–5**. Mids: **5–7**. Bass: **4–6**.
- Pick attack: **soft**, often closer to fingerstyle than hard picking.

If it sounds like a normal guitar with chorus, push the filter or pitch movement slightly. If it stops feeling playable, back off.

## What’s Going On Musically

The guitar role is part harmony, part hook, part texture. The lines often use simple melodic cells, but the processing bends their identity. A small slide or bend can feel like a synth portamento. A sustained double-stop can feel like a pad.

Harmonically, the lesson is less about a formal jazz map and more about **ambiguous color**: suspended tones, close intervals, and phrases that do not always resolve where classic rock expects. The guitar is allowed to lean against the track.

The touch is crucial. Processed tones can make players overplay because every note sounds interesting. This style works better when you play fewer notes and let the processing complete the sentence.

## The Drills

Original exercises for the processed-guitar approach.

**Drill 1 — Portamento Bend Control.** Target: **68 BPM**. Bend like a synth, not a blues lick.

```
Use compression and slight vibrato modulation.

e|----------------------------|
B|--5b7~~~~--7r5--------------|
G|--------------6--4----------|
D|-------------------6--------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
```

*Builds:* smooth pitch travel. Keep the bend slow and even.

**Drill 2 — Filtered Double-Stops.** Target: **76 BPM**. Let close intervals bloom.

```
Neck pickup, filter low-to-medium.

e|----------------------------|
B|--5--5/7--7--7\5------------|
G|--6--6/8--8--8\6------------|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
```

*Builds:* intonation inside processed harmony. If the modulation makes it sour, reduce depth.

**Drill 3 — Space After Texture.** Target: **60 BPM**. Play a phrase, then let the chain decay.

```
Count two beats of silence after each phrase.

e|----------------|----------------|
B|--7~~--5--------|----------------|
G|---------6--4---|----------------|
D|--------------6-|----------------|
A|----------------|----------------|
E|----------------|----------------|
   phrase          let processor speak
```

*Builds:* restraint. Do not fill the decay just because the sound is interesting.

## Make It Yours

Try building a tone where the first question is not “which amp,” but “what happens after I play the note.” Add one processor at a time: compression, then pitch movement, then filter, then saturation. Stop when the guitar starts to argue with the keyboard sounds but still obeys your hands. That tension is the lesson.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20:** guitar-like attack wrapped in synth-like movement.
> - **Vocal spaces:** small phrases that leave room for the processing to decay.
> - **Hook returns:** bends that glide rather than snap.
> - **Layered sections:** guitar and synth timbres crossing into each other’s territory.
> - **Final repeats:** instability that feels musical, not accidental.
