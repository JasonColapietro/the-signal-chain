# Lesson 82 — “Song to the Siren” · Tim Buckley

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Tim Buckley
- **Song:** “Song to the Siren”
- **Album:** *Starsailor* (1970)
- **The tone in one sentence:** A spare, hovering guitar accompaniment where open space, light vibrato, and held chords do more emotional work than a busy part ever could.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** D-centered for this lesson; standard tuning (E A D G B E). Recorded and cover-version approaches vary, so treat the key here as a playable study frame.
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

This is not a gear-chase song. The important sound is **space**: a guitar part that leaves enough air for the voice to bend around it.

- **Guitar:** The exact guitar on the *Starsailor* recording is not something I would claim with certainty. A lightly played steel-string acoustic, a 12-string-like shimmer, or a clean electric with open voicings can all teach the right lesson.
- **Pickup/recording:** If acoustic, think natural body and string noise rather than glossy pickup quack. If electric, think neck or middle pickup through a clean amp with the tone softened.
- **Effects:** Any ambience should be modest and room-like. A small amount of plate or room reverb can help the sustain, but a long modern wash can hide the touch.
- **Studio detail:** The reliable detail is the restraint. Exact mic choices and guitar model claims are not documented well enough for this lesson, so the recipe stays behavioral.

The cross-reference is personal as much as musical: Tim Buckley is Jeff Buckley's father, and the brief points forward to the Jeff Buckley lessons later in the expansion. Keep that family line in mind when you hear how much guitar accompaniment can be carried by breath and space.

## The Tone Recipe

**The record path, cautiously stated:** sparse acoustic or clean-guitar accompaniment, light room around it, and a vocal-centered arrangement where the guitar supports rather than competes.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any steel-string acoustic, small-body folk guitar, clean hollowbody, or Strat/Tele neck pickup.
- **Amp:** If electric, use a clean amp or model with headroom. If acoustic, play into the room before you add anything.
- **Reverb:** Room or plate in a low range. You want a halo, not fog.
- **Pick or fingers:** Fingers make the attack rounder. A thin pick can work if you play near the neck and soften the stroke.

**Starting points (all approximate ranges):**

- Acoustic touch: light-to-medium, with the thumb quieter than the top strings.
- Electric EQ: Bass **4-5**, mids **5-6**, treble **4-6**.
- Reverb: **2-4**, short decay.
- Guitar tone: **6-8** if electric.
- Vibrato: slow and narrow on held notes, more like a singer holding pitch than a blues shake.

## What's Going On Musically

For this lesson, think in a **D-centered major color** with suspended notes. A suspended chord replaces the third with the second or fourth. That matters here because a normal major or minor chord tells the listener exactly how to feel. A suspended chord withholds that answer.

The part works by avoiding overstatement. Let open strings ring, use small upper-string movements, and leave whole beats empty. The guitar is not trying to explain the song. It is creating a shoreline for the vocal to move against.

The main devices:

- **Open-string sustain:** fretted notes move while open strings keep the air vibrating.
- **Suspensions:** 2nds and 4ths hold back the major/minor answer.
- **Vibrato as accompaniment:** a held note with slow movement can replace a strum.
- **Dynamic patience:** the quietest note in the bar may be the one that makes the next phrase land.

That restraint is hard for guitar players because it feels like doing less. In this song, doing less is the arrangement.

## The Drills

**Drill A — Leave the Bar Open**

Trains you to stop filling every beat. Count the rests out loud.

```
~60 BPM. Play the note, then let the rest do its job.

e|--------0---------------0-----|
B|------3---3-----------3-------|
G|----2-------2---------------0-|
D|--0----------------0---------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   sound      rest    sound
```

*Builds: patience, sustain control, and comfort with empty space.*

**Drill B — Suspended-Color Turn**

Builds clean movement between 2nds, 3rds, and 4ths without making the part busy.

```
~66 BPM. Keep every note soft and even.

e|--0-----0-----0-----0---------|
B|--3-----2-----3-----1---------|
G|--2-----2-----0-----0---------|
D|--0-----0-----0-----0---------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   sus   major sus   soft fall
```

*Builds: hearing how one moving voice changes the mood while the hand barely moves.*

**Drill C — Slow Vibrato Accompaniment**

Trains vibrato as support rather than lead playing.

```
~54 BPM. One held note per bar; vibrato starts late.

e|------------------------------|
B|--3~~~~~~~~~~~---1~~~~--------|
G|------------------------2~~~~-|
D|------------------------------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   wait, then move
```

*Builds: controlled, delayed vibrato. Strike the note, let it settle, then add movement from the wrist.*

## Make It Yours

Write an accompaniment where the rule is simple: no more than three notes in any two beats. That constraint will force you to choose which note actually matters. Use suspended shapes, let open strings carry the sustain, and make vibrato part of the chord instead of a lead ornament.

The deeper move is trust. A guitar can frame a voice without announcing itself. When the song already has ache in the melody, your job may be to hold the lantern, not tell the story twice.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:00:** the amount of air before the vocal fully settles in.
> - **Around 0:30:** guitar support that avoids crowding the phrase endings.
> - **Around 1:05:** held tones acting like emotional punctuation.
> - **Around 1:45:** the way the accompaniment stays small even as the singing opens up.
> - **Throughout:** vibrato and space doing the work of extra chords.
