# Lesson 83 — “Since I've Been Loving You” · Led Zeppelin

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Led Zeppelin
- **Song:** “Since I've Been Loving You”
- **Album:** *Led Zeppelin III* (1970)
- **The tone in one sentence:** Jimmy Page's blues voice at its most dynamic — a bright humbucker or single-coil pushed into singing breakup, clean enough for whispers and hot enough to scream when the hand digs in.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** C minor blues; standard tuning (E A D G B E)
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

This is Page as a player-historian himself: B.B. King-style space, British amp bite, and a studio take that keeps the human unevenness.

- **Guitar:** Page is most associated in this era with a **Gibson Les Paul** and earlier with the **Dragon Telecaster**. For this specific recording, the exact guitar claim is commonly debated in the usual Page way, so I would describe the target tone by behavior: bright bridge pickup, strong midrange, and a vocal upper register.
- **Amp:** A pushed tube amp is the safe center of the sound. Marshall-style volume and small-amp compression are both plausible references in Page lore; exact model and settings are not a settled public fact.
- **Gain:** Natural amp breakup, not modern saturation. The quiet phrases should still clean up under the fingers.
- **Studio detail:** The track is famous for performance detail more than documented knob detail. Any precise mic or dial claim should be treated as approximate.

Read this next to Lessons 16 and 17: those Zeppelin lessons show Page as riff architect and long-form arranger, while this one puts the microscope on touch.

## The Tone Recipe

**The record path, cautiously stated:** bright electric guitar, likely a bridge or cutting pickup voice, into a tube amp pushed into expressive breakup, with enough room around the notes to hear every scrape and dynamic change.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Les Paul-style bridge humbucker, Tele bridge pickup, or Strat bridge-plus-middle if you need more edge.
- **Amp:** Marshall-, Supro-, or Vox-style crunch model with the gain in the middle range.
- **Pedals:** A low-gain overdrive can stand in if your amp stays too clean. Avoid heavy compression.
- **Reverb:** Short room or spring in a low range.

**Starting points (all approximate ranges):**

- Gain: **4-6**, with sustain from volume and touch rather than a pedal doing all the work.
- Amp EQ: Bass **3-5**, mids **6-8**, treble **5-7**.
- Guitar volume: **7-10** as a performance control.
- Pick attack: fingers or pick both work; what matters is the ability to go from near-clean to biting within one phrase.
- Vibrato: wide, slow, and deliberate on held notes.

## What's Going On Musically

The song is a **slow C minor blues**. The core scale is **C minor pentatonic** (C-Eb-F-G-Bb), with the blues note Gb available for tension. Page also aims hard at chord tones, especially when the harmony moves. That is why the solo sounds like speech instead of a scale exercise.

A slow blues leaves nowhere to hide. At this tempo, a bend that is a little sharp or flat stays in the listener's ear. The lesson is not speed. It is **dynamic control**: one phrase whispered, the next phrase nearly breaking the speaker.

The organ and guitar also matter together. The guitar is not filling every measure. It answers, complains, waits, and then cuts through. That is classic blues conversation: the band states the room, the guitar says one sentence, then the singer answers.

The most useful concept here is **target-note bending**. You do not just bend because bends sound bluesy. You bend to a pitch that belongs to the chord, hold it, and prove you can keep it there.

## Signature Moves

These are short study fragments in Page's blues vocabulary, not a full transcription.

**1. The C-minor cry**

```
Feel: slow blues, ~58 BPM. Bend to pitch, then wait.

e|------------------------------|
B|--11b13~~~---11--8------------|
G|-------------------10b12r10---|
D|------------------------------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   reach       answer
```

*Caption: The first bend has to arrive in tune before the vibrato starts. Do not shake your way into pitch.*

**2. Quiet-to-loud answer**

```
Feel: start soft, finish hard.

e|------------------------------|
B|---------------------8--------|
G|--8h10--8------8h10-----10~---|
D|----------10------------------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   whisper       push
```

*Caption: Same box, different hand pressure. The gain should respond to the pick, not stay flat.*

**3. Minor-blues turnaround tag**

```
Feel: behind the beat, let the last note hang.

e|------------------------------|
B|--8---------------------------|
G|----10--8---------------------|
D|----------10--8---------------|
A|---------------10--8--6~------|
E|------------------------------|
   fall through the box
```

*Caption: A descending phrase is only useful if it lands somewhere. Make the last note sound chosen.*

## The Drills

**Drill A — Target Bend Check**

Trains pitch accuracy before vibrato.

```
~54 BPM. Play the target note first, then bend to match it.

e|------------------------------|
B|--13----11b13~~~---13---------|
G|------------------------------|
D|------------------------------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   target bend       compare
```

*Builds: ear-first bending. If the bent note does not match the fretted 13th fret, slow down.*

**Drill B — Dynamic Three-Sentence Blues**

Trains the soft-medium-hard arc inside one chorus.

```
~58 BPM. Phrase 1 soft, phrase 2 medium, phrase 3 hard.

e|----------------|----------------|----------------|
B|----------------|--8-------------|--11b13~~--8----|
G|--8h10--8-------|----10--8-------|-------------10-|
D|----------10~---|----------10~---|----------------|
A|----------------|----------------|----------------|
E|----------------|----------------|----------------|
   soft             medium           hard
```

*Builds: controlling gain with the hand. Do not touch the amp between phrases.*

**Drill C — Slow-Blues Space**

Trains leaving room after a phrase.

```
~50 BPM. Play two beats, rest two beats.

e|------------------------------|
B|--8------11b13~---------------|
G|----10----------(rest)--------|
D|-----------------------10~----|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   phrase        silence  answer
```

*Builds: restraint. Count the rest as loudly as you count the notes.*

## Make It Yours

Take one minor-pentatonic box and ban yourself from playing more than five notes per phrase. Then make those notes bigger with dynamics, bends, and silence. That is the real Page lesson here. The same C minor box can sound small or enormous depending on how much air you leave around it.

Also practice using your guitar volume knob. Set the amp so the loudest phrase is almost too hot, then roll the guitar down for the quiet passages. If the tone does not change under your hand, the rig is too compressed for this song.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:00:** the dry, exposed guitar entrance and how much touch you can hear.
> - **Around 0:45:** the way Page lets the organ and vocal own space before answering.
> - **Around 2:00:** bends that bloom into controlled vibrato rather than nervous shaking.
> - **Around 4:00:** the gain opening up when the playing gets harder.
> - **Throughout:** dynamics as the real arrangement, not decoration.
