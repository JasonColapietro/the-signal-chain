# Lesson 106 — “Weird Fishes/Arpeggi” · Radiohead

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Radiohead
- **Song:** “Weird Fishes/Arpeggi”
- **Album:** *In Rainbows* (2007)
- **The tone in one sentence:** Clean guitars woven into a moving arpeggio lattice, where the shimmer comes from interlocking hands more than from any one effect.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** E-minor / E-Dorian-colored center; standard tuning, E A D G B E.
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Radiohead already appears in Lesson 46 with “Paranoid Android”; this lesson is the gentler cousin, where the band uses interlocking clean parts instead of violent contrast.

The exact *In Rainbows* guitar chain is not published in full studio detail, and Radiohead’s parts are often layered across Jonny Greenwood, Ed O’Brien, and Thom Yorke. The useful truth is broader:

- **Guitars:** Jonny Greenwood is commonly associated with **Telecaster-style instruments**, and Ed O’Brien with a range of textural Fender-style guitars. Treat the exact guitar on each overdub as uncertain; you want a clear single-coil or split-coil sound that stays even under fast picking.
- **Amps:** Clean **Fender / Vox / studio-clean platforms** are a reasonable family to work from. The amp should not sag or distort when the pattern gets busy.
- **Effects:** Light compression, a little reverb, and possibly subtle delay can help, but the arpeggio itself is the effect. If a delay pedal is doing more work than your right hand, the part loses its human weave.
- **Layering:** Multiple guitars and patterns create the motion. No single isolated part tells the whole story.

The signal chain here is almost architectural: one clean line, then another clean line, then another, until the track starts to move like water.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** bright, controlled clean guitars, lightly processed and layered into a pulse. Exact pedals and amp settings are not settled publicly, so aim for clarity, sustain, and separation.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Strat, Tele, Jazzmaster, PRS split coil, or any clean guitar with good note separation.
- **Amp/model:** Clean Fender, Vox clean, or a neutral modeler patch.
- **Compression:** Light, slow enough that the pick attack remains.
- **Reverb:** Plate or room, low-to-medium mix.
- **Delay:** Optional eighth-note or dotted-eighth texture, very low mix. The picked pattern should still be the clock.

**Starting ranges (out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **2-3**, bass **4-5**, mids **5-6**, treble **5-7**.
- Compression: sustain **2-4**, level matched.
- Reverb: **2-4**.
- Delay mix, if used: **1-2**, feedback low.
- Pick attack: small, even alternate picking. No heroic accents unless the arrangement asks for them.

Use less effect than you think. The interlock gets blurry fast.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Weird Fishes/Arpeggi” is built from **arpeggios** — chords broken into individual notes — but the magic is that the parts do not all start and stop in the same emotional place. Each guitar repeats a small cell, and the band layers those cells until the harmony feels like it is continuously turning.

The tonal center hovers around **E minor**, with Dorian-like brightness when the harmony lifts. Dorian means a minor sound with a natural 6th, which gives minor music a less gloomy, more suspended color. You do not have to name every chord to feel it: the bass and vocal move through a minor world while the guitars keep sparkling above.

The core musical skills are:

- **Even alternate picking:** every note the same size.
- **String crossing:** moving across strings without accents poking out.
- **Layer awareness:** playing one small part as if two other parts are already beside it.
- **Crescendo by density:** the arrangement grows because more patterns join, not because one guitar suddenly stomps on a distortion pedal.

This is difficult because it is exposed. Clean, fast, quiet guitar leaves nowhere to hide.

## The Drills

These are original exercises for clean arpeggio layering.

**Drill 1 — Even Six-Note Cell.** Trains the basic arpeggio engine without rushing.

```
BPM target: 126, then 152. Alternate pick every note.

e|---------7-----------7-----------7----------|
B|-----8-----8-----8-----8-----8-----8-------|
G|---9---9-------9---9-------9---9-----------|
D|-------------------------------------------|
A|-------------------------------------------|
E|-------------------------------------------|
```

*Builds: evenness. Record it clean; if the string crossings sound louder than the rest, slow down.*

**Drill 2 — Two-Layer Simulation.** Trains one guitarist to imply two parts by changing only the top note.

```
BPM target: 138. Repeat each bar four times.

e|---------7-----------7--|---------10-----------10--|
B|-----8-----8-----8------|-----8------8-----8-------|
G|---9---9-------9---9----|---9---9--------9---9-----|
D|------------------------|--------------------------|
A|------------------------|--------------------------|
E|------------------------|--------------------------|
```

*Builds: arrangement memory. The hand pattern stays the same while the harmony color shifts.*

**Drill 3 — Clean Crescendo.** Trains getting bigger without getting dirty or uneven.

```
BPM target: 144. Play four repeats, p to mf.

e|---------7-----------8-----------10---------|
B|-----8-----8-----8-----8-----8------8-------|
G|---9---9-------9---9-------9---9------------|
D|--------------------------------------------|
A|--------------------------------------------|
E|--------------------------------------------|
```

*Builds: dynamic control. Increase volume by widening the pick stroke slightly, not by tensing the wrist.*

## Make It Yours

Build your own lattice with one small arpeggio cell. Keep the picking pattern identical and change one note every four bars. Then record a second pass that starts in a different register. You will hear the Radiohead lesson immediately: complexity can come from patient layering, not from difficult chords. If your clean tone feels too plain, resist the urge to add more pedals first. Add a second part, a shared top note, or a small rhythmic offset. The arrangement is the shimmer.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00-0:30:** the first clean arpeggios establishing pulse before the arrangement blooms.
> - **Verse sections:** repeated guitar cells staying almost machine-even while the vocal floats.
> - **Middle build:** new layers creating lift without a big distortion change.
> - **Late-song release:** the guitars still pulsing while the harmony opens underneath.
> - **Throughout:** clean note separation; no single picked note should bark louder than the pattern.
