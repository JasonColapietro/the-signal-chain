# Lesson 76 — “Like a Rolling Stone” · Bob Dylan (Mike Bloomfield)

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Bob Dylan (Mike Bloomfield on lead guitar)
- **Song:** “Like a Rolling Stone”
- **Album:** *Highway 61 Revisited* (1965)
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES
- **The tone in one sentence:** A bright, wiry electric guitar stepping into a folk song and making the whole track feel newly dangerous.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** C major; standard tuning.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

This is the going-electric Telecaster lesson, but with the usual caution: the record is better documented as a cultural moment than as a complete guitar-rig diagram.

- **Guitar:** Mike Bloomfield is commonly associated with a Fender Telecaster on these sessions. The useful target is a bright bridge-pickup electric with quick attack and not much extra sustain.
- **Amp:** A Fender-style clean-to-edge amp is the most sensible reference point, but exact amp model, settings, mic, and placement are not reliably published at the level of a modern session recall.
- **Effects:** None need to be part of the core recipe. If there is room sound, it is not a pedalboard sound.
- **Role:** The guitar is not trying to dominate the organ, piano, vocal, and rhythm section. It cuts through by choosing the right register and answering at the right time.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** a bright single-coil electric into a clean or barely pushed amp. The attack should be immediate, and the note should get out of the way fast enough for the lyric and organ to keep moving.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Telecaster bridge pickup is ideal. A Strat bridge or any bright single-coil works. A humbucker guitar can get close if you split coils or lower the tone slightly but keep the attack clear.
- **Amp:** Clean Fender-style model, small combo, or any clean platform with a little edge.
- **Drive:** Optional low overdrive if your amp is too polite.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **2–4**.
- Bass: **3–5**. Mids: **5–6**. Treble: **6–8**.
- Reverb: **0–2**.
- Guitar tone: **7–10**.
- Pick attack: **light-to-medium**, with accents saved for fills.

The guitar should feel like a speaking voice in a crowded room: bright enough to be heard, not so loud that it ruins the conversation.

## What’s Going On Musically

The song is in **C major**, and the famous lift comes from simple chords moving with enormous confidence. The guitar’s job is to decorate that movement with short answers: double-stops, little pentatonic turns, and clipped fills around the vocal.

The lead vocabulary is mostly major pentatonic and chord-tone targeting. Over C, that means C, D, E, G, and A, with passing notes used as color. Bloomfield’s blues background matters, but he is not pouring a club solo over the track. He is adding electricity to the arrangement.

That is the lesson: sometimes the right electric guitar part is not bigger, faster, or dirtier. It is the part that changes the temperature of the whole band.

## The Drills

Original exercises for the arrangement role.

**Drill 1 — Two-Beat Fill Discipline.** Target: **92 BPM**. Fill only the space you are given.

```
Play the fill, then rest.

e|----------------|----------------|
B|--5--6--5-------|----------------|
G|----------5--7--|----------------|
D|----------------|----------------|
A|----------------|----------------|
E|----------------|----------------|
   fill             stay out
```

*Builds:* restraint. The empty bar is part of the exercise.

**Drill 2 — Major Double-Stop Walk.** Target: **100 BPM**. Move sweet two-note shapes cleanly.

```
Bright pickup, light attack.

e|--3--5--7--8--7--5----------|
B|--5--6--8--8--8--6----------|
G|----------------------------|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
```

*Builds:* major-key double-stop movement without blues overstatement.

**Drill 3 — Organ-Space Comping.** Target: **88 BPM**. Leave holes for the rest of the band.

```
Short C and F fragments; mute immediately.

e|--3--x------5--x------------|
B|--5--x------6--x------------|
G|--5--x------5--x------------|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
   C stab    F stab
```

*Builds:* clipped arrangement playing. Do not let the chord wash into the next instrument’s space.

## Make It Yours

Put a bright electric guitar into any acoustic song you know, but give yourself a rule: no full strumming for the first verse. Only answer phrases, two-note shapes, and short chord stabs. You will hear quickly whether your part is helping the song lift or just adding noise. Bloomfield’s lesson here is courage with restraint.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20:** electric guitar entering as part of a crowded arrangement, not above it.
> - **Verse gaps:** clipped fills answering the vocal.
> - **Chorus lift:** bright guitar color helping the track widen.
> - **Organ-heavy sections:** guitar staying out of the way until its register matters.
> - **Final choruses:** the electric edge making the folk structure feel unstable in the best way.
