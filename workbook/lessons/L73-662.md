# Lesson 73 — “662” · Christone “Kingfish” Ingram

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Christone “Kingfish” Ingram
- **Song:** “662”
- **Album:** *662* (2021)
- **Section:** NOW
- **The tone in one sentence:** Present-day electric blues with old roots: thick single-note sustain, a firm right hand, and enough modern gain to sing without hiding the phrasing.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** Blues-rock vocabulary centered here in G for the workbook; standard tuning. Move the shapes to match any live version or reference pitch you use.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Kingfish is the book’s blues spine brought into the present. You can hear the older language — B.B., Albert, Freddie, Hendrix, Stevie — but the gain structure and attack belong to a player raised with all of that already in the rearview mirror.

- **Guitar:** Ingram is commonly associated with Fender-style guitars, including Telecaster Deluxe-style instruments with fuller pickups in recent years. The exact session guitar for this track is not something I would state as settled, so aim for a clear guitar that can produce a fat bridge or neck-lead voice.
- **Amp:** A pushed tube-amp or modern blues amp platform is the broad target. Exact amp, speaker, and mic details are not the point I would hang the lesson on.
- **Drive:** Medium overdrive is enough. The sound should sustain, but you still need to hear pick attack and left-hand vibrato.
- **Effects:** Any ambience should be modest. A little room or spring-style reverb helps, but a big wash softens the authority of the bends.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** a strong guitar signal into a singing blues-rock drive, with mids forward and the low end controlled. The note should stay alive during bends, but the rig should clean up when you back off.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Tele Deluxe, Strat, Les Paul, SG, or any guitar with a clear bridge pickup. A neck pickup works for the rounder phrases if it does not get woolly.
- **Drive:** Tube Screamer-style, Blues Driver-style, Klon-style, or an amp’s own pushed channel.
- **Amp:** Fender-style clean platform, British-style crunch, or a blues amp model. Keep the mids audible.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Drive: **4–6**.
- Tone: **4–6**, adjusted so bends stay vocal rather than sharp.
- Amp bass: **4–5**. Mids: **6–8**. Treble: **5–7**.
- Guitar volume: **7–10**, using the knob to move from rhythm to lead.
- Pick attack: **medium-hard**, with relaxed time. Do not rush the bend just because the gain will sustain it.

## What’s Going On Musically

“662” sits in the long blues conversation, but it does not sound like museum work. The language is still built from minor pentatonic, major/minor 3rd friction, dominant chords, and call-and-response phrasing. The difference is the **density of attack**: the phrases are bigger, the amp is thicker, and the bends carry a modern sustain.

The most important concept is still the **blue third**. In G, that means rubbing B♭ against B natural. Bend the minor 3rd toward the major 3rd, then decide whether to land sweet or keep the note crying. That one move ties this lesson back to the early blues and forward to the current record.

Form-wise, think riff-based blues-rock rather than a polite 12-bar exercise. The vocal, guitar, and rhythm section trade authority. Your job is to make short phrases sound like answers, not just scale fragments.

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style.

**Drill 1 — Blue-Third Accuracy.** Target: **72 BPM**. Bend the minor 3rd toward the major 3rd without overshooting.

```
G blues. Sing the target before you bend.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|--3b4--3b4--3--5--3---------|
D|--------------------5-------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
   Bb->B        resolve
```

*Builds:* intonation and blues color. Use less gain than you want until the pitch is honest.

**Drill 2 — Riff-to-Lead Call.** Target: **88 BPM**. Alternate a low riff and a high answer.

```
Keep the riff firm and the answer vocal.

e|----------------|----------------|
B|----------------|--6b8~~--6--3---|
G|----------------|--------------5-|
D|----------3--5--|----------------|
A|----3--5--------|----------------|
E|--3-------------|----------------|
   CALL             RESPONSE
```

*Builds:* phrasing across registers. The two bars should sound like one conversation.

**Drill 3 — Sustain Without Hiding.** Target: **60 BPM**. Hold notes long enough to expose your vibrato.

```
One phrase, wide vibrato only after the pitch settles.

e|----------------------------|
B|--8~~~--6-------------------|
G|----------7--5b7r5--3-------|
D|----------------------5-----|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
```

*Builds:* controlled sustain, release, and vibrato. The gain helps, but your hand still has to carry the note.

## Make It Yours

Take the oldest blues move you know and play it with a current gain structure. Then do the reverse: take a modern high-sustain lead tone and make it obey old blues phrasing. Kingfish sits right in that overlap. The line has to sing, but it also has to answer the groove. That is the thread from the early lessons to now.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20:** the guitar’s thick attack, not just its sustain.
> - **Vocal gaps:** short lead answers that feel spoken back to the singer.
> - **Solo sections:** bends that reach pitch before the vibrato starts.
> - **Riff returns:** low-register blues phrases carrying as much weight as high ones.
> - **Final choruses:** gain staying clear enough that the hands remain audible.
