# Lesson 78 — “Brown Eyed Girl” · Van Morrison

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Van Morrison
- **Song:** “Brown Eyed Girl”
- **Album:** *Blowin' Your Mind!* (1967)
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES
- **The tone in one sentence:** Bright, clean double-stops over simple major chords — one of the first hooks that teaches players how two notes can become a whole memory.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** G major; standard tuning.

## The Rig & Signal Chain

This is a lesson where the part is far more documented in players' hands than the exact studio rig is in public records. The hook matters more than the equipment receipt.

- **Guitar:** A bright clean electric is the honest target. Specific guitar claims for the session are not reliable enough for me to build the lesson around one model.
- **Amp:** A clean small-to-medium combo sound works. Think clear, present, and not heavily compressed.
- **Effects:** Little to none for the core part. A small amount of room or spring-style reverb is fine if it stays out of the way.
- **Role:** The guitar hook is a melodic signpost. It does not need gain, speed, or thickness. It needs clean intonation and happy time.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** clean electric guitar, bright but not sharp, with the two-note hook sitting above the band. The notes should separate cleanly and decay naturally.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Strat, Tele, semi-hollow, or any clean electric. Use bridge-plus-middle, middle, or a bright neck setting depending on the guitar.
- **Amp:** Clean combo or modeler clean patch.
- **Reverb:** A little room if the sound is too dry.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **1–3**.
- Bass: **3–5**. Mids: **5–6**. Treble: **5–7**.
- Reverb: **1–3**.
- Guitar tone: **7–10**.
- Pick attack: **light, even, and slightly bouncing**.

The double-stops should feel effortless. If they sound harsh, lower treble before you soften the rhythm.

## What’s Going On Musically

The song is in **G major**, and the basic chord loop is one of the friendliest in guitar music: G, C, G, D. The hook uses **thirds** — two-note shapes that move through the major scale. A third is simply two notes separated by three letter names, like G to B or A to C.

Thirds sound complete without becoming full chords. That is why this little figure works so well for beginners: you are playing melody and harmony at the same time, but your fretting hand only has to manage two strings.

The deeper lesson is **voice leading**. Each two-note shape moves to the next by small steps. Nothing jumps far, so the hook feels like it is smiling its way through the chord changes.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments only.

**1. The major double-stop hook**

```
Feel: bright 8ths, ~150 BPM. Let pairs ring cleanly.

e|--3--5--7--8--7--5--3-------|
B|--3--5--8--8--8--5--3-------|
G|----------------------------|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
   G major thirds
```

*Caption:* Two strings, one hook. Keep both notes equal.

**2. The G-C-G-D map**

```
Feel: light strum, no heavy attack.

e|--3----0----3----2----------|
B|--3----1----3----3----------|
G|--0----0----0----2----------|
D|--0----2----0----0----------|
A|--2----3----2---------------|
E|--3---------3---------------|
   G    C    G    D
```

*Caption:* Simple chords under the hook. The rhythm has to stay buoyant.

**3. Lower-string answer**

```
Feel: short and clean.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|--4--5--7--5--4-------------|
D|--5--7--9--7--5-------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
   same idea, lower register
```

*Caption:* Move the double-stop idea down and it becomes an answer phrase.

## The Drills

Original exercises for the hook.

**Drill 1 — Thirds in G.** Target: **96 BPM**. Move through the scale in two-note shapes.

```
Play evenly; no rushing the top.

e|--3--5--7--8--10--12--------|
B|--3--5--8--10--12--13-------|
G|----------------------------|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
```

*Builds:* clean double-stop fingering and major-scale harmony.

**Drill 2 — Hook Then Chord.** Target: **88 BPM**. Land the hook into the chord underneath.

```
Two beats hook, two beats chord.

e|--3--5--7--8--|--3-----------|
B|--3--5--8--8--|--3-----------|
G|--------------|--0-----------|
D|--------------|--0-----------|
A|--------------|--2-----------|
E|--------------|--3-----------|
                 G
```

*Builds:* connecting lead fragments to rhythm guitar.

**Drill 3 — Double-Stop Timekeeper.** Target: **112 BPM**. Keep the bounce steady.

```
Accent beats 2 and 4 lightly.

e|--3--3--5--5--7--7--5--5----|
B|--3--3--5--5--8--8--5--5----|
G|----------------------------|
D|----------------------------|
A|----------------------------|
E|----------------------------|
```

*Builds:* even attack and bright rhythm feel. Do not let the pairs drag.

## Make It Yours

Take any I-IV-V song in a major key and write a two-string hook before you write a solo. Start with thirds on the top two strings, then move the same shapes to the G and B strings. You will hear why this part became every beginner’s friend: it sounds musical immediately, but it also teaches harmony quietly.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:10:** the double-stop hook stating the whole mood.
> - **Verse entrances:** clean guitar staying light under the vocal.
> - **Chord loop returns:** G-C-G-D feeling like a circle rather than a sequence.
> - **Hook repeats:** both notes in each pair ringing evenly.
> - **Later choruses:** the part staying bright without getting louder or dirtier.
