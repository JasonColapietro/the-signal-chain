# Lesson 98 — “Angeles” · Elliott Smith

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Elliott Smith
- **Song:** “Angeles”
- **Album:** *Either/Or* (1997)
- **The tone in one sentence:** A close, double-tracked fingerpicked whisper where the guitar sounds less like accompaniment and more like a second nervous system.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** C/A-minor family of shapes; standard tuning for this lesson
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Elliott Smith's guitar sound is intimate enough that people often confuse the instrument with the recording method. The exact "Angeles" chain is not documented in a way that supports hard claims about microphones or preamps, so keep the rig description modest.

- **Guitar:** Smith is commonly associated with small-to-mid-sized steel-string acoustics, including Yamaha FG-family instruments across his catalog. For this lesson, the important qualities are quick attack, balanced midrange, and a top that does not boom.
- **Performance:** Double-tracking is a major part of the sound. The small timing differences between two takes create width without chorus.
- **Mic/room:** Close microphone placement is the practical target. Exact mic models for this track should be treated as uncertain unless a session source is in front of you.
- **Effects:** Little obvious effect. Any compression should behave like tape or console leveling, not a modern squashed acoustic pop sound.

The tone is quiet, but it is not casual. Every fingertip is under a microscope.

## The Tone Recipe

You can approximate it with one acoustic and careful recording, or with a clean electric if that is what you have.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Steel-string acoustic, parlor, OM, or any balanced dreadnought played softly. A nylon-string will be warmer but less like the record.
- **Pickup/mic:** A close condenser-style mic, phone mic placed carefully, or acoustic DI blended with room if you have no mic.
- **Double-track:** Record the part twice instead of adding chorus.
- **Compression:** Light, slow, and only enough to catch peaks.

**Starting points:**

- Pick/fingers: fingers only, nails short-to-medium, thumb steady.
- EQ: reduce boomy lows around the body of the guitar before adding treble.
- Reverb: off or very short room.
- Double-track pan: modest left/right spread, not hard novelty stereo.
- Attack: brush into the string, not away from it.

If the part sounds impressive, you may be playing it too loud. It should feel like you are leaning toward the microphone.

## What's Going On Musically

"Angeles" is a fingerpicking lesson disguised as a song. The harmony moves through folk-pop shapes, but the real difficulty is **independence**: the thumb carries a bass motion while the fingers outline upper chord tones and little melodic turns.

The emotional color comes from **voice-leading**. A note on top stays still while the bass moves, or an inner voice falls by step while the chord name changes underneath. That is why the song feels harmonically richer than a basic strum sheet would suggest.

The double-tracked performance adds another layer of discipline. Two slightly different takes make the guitar wider, but only if both takes are steady. If the time wobbles, double-tracking turns intimacy into clutter.

## The Drills

Original exercises for the picking-hand vocabulary.

**Drill A — Thumb Independence (~76 BPM)**  
Keep the bass steady while the fingers move a small melody.

```
e|--------0-----------0-----|
B|------1---1-------3---1---|
G|----0-------0---0-------0-|
D|--------------------------|
A|--3-----------3-----------|
E|--------------------------|
   thumb holds C
```

*Builds: bass stability under a moving top line.*

**Drill B — Double-Track Grid (~88 BPM)**  
Practice a part clean enough to record twice without drifting.

```
e|------0-------0-------1-------0---|
B|----1---1---1---1---1---1---1---1-|
G|--0-------2-------2-------0-------|
D|----------------------------------|
A|--3-------0----------------3------|
E|------------------1---------------|
   take one clean, then repeat
```

*Builds: repeatability. The second pass should feel like a shadow, not a correction.*

**Drill C — Whisper Dynamics (~72 BPM)**  
Play the same figure three times, each softer than the last.

```
e|--------0-----------0-----|
B|------1---1-------1---1---|
G|----0-------0---0-------0-|
D|--------------------------|
A|--3-----------2-----------|
E|--------------------------|
   mf          mp          p
```

*Builds: control at low volume. Quiet playing still needs time and tone.*

## Make It Yours

The portable move is double-tracked fingerpicking. Write a four-chord pattern, record it once, then record it again without listening to the first take too loudly. Do not quantize the life out of it. Let the tiny differences make the width. Then practice the same part at whisper volume until every note still has a beginning, a center, and an end. That is the Elliott Smith lesson: intimacy is not looseness. It is precision played softly.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00–0:20, roughly:** close fingerpicked guitar with very little studio gloss.
> - **Verse movement:** bass notes walking under quiet upper voices.
> - **Double-tracked width:** two performances widening the guitar without obvious modulation.
> - **String noise:** small human sounds left in, not scrubbed away.
> - **Overall:** difficult guitar presented as a whisper instead of a display.
