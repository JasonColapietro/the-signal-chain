# Lesson 67 — “Reptilia” · The Strokes

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** The Strokes (Nick Valensi and Albert Hammond Jr.)
- **Song:** “Reptilia”
- **Album:** *Room on Fire* (2003)
- **Section:** THE 2000s
- **The tone in one sentence:** Two clipped, compressed guitar voices interlocking like small amps pushed just hard enough to bite.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** B minor-centered rock; standard tuning, E A D G B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

The Strokes guitar sound is not about size. It is about two parts staying narrow, bright, and rhythmically useful so the band can move as one machine.

- **Guitars:** **Nick Valensi’s Epiphone Riviera/P-94-style voice** and **Albert Hammond Jr.'s Strat-style voice** are commonly cited as the band’s core contrast: one thicker and more mid-forward, one brighter and more wiry.
- **Amps:** Small-to-mid **Fender combo behavior** is commonly associated with the early Strokes sound. The target is compressed clean-to-crunch, not a huge high-gain stack.
- **Effects:** The recorded parts sound comparatively dry. Compression and amp breakup are more important than obvious modulation.
- **Gain:** Low-to-moderate. Enough hair to flatten the attack slightly, but not enough to hide the interlocking lines.
- **Mic/room:** Exact mic recipes are not the lesson. The useful behavior is close, controlled, and narrow enough for two guitars to share the middle.

The arrangement only works if both players leave room. If one guitar gets too wide or too distorted, the puzzle stops fitting.

## The Tone Recipe

Think two smaller sounds, not one giant one.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar 1:** Any P-90 or humbucker guitar for the thicker part.
- **Guitar 2:** Any Strat/Tele-style single-coil guitar for the brighter part.
- **Amp:** A clean Fender-style combo or modeler with gain pushed into light crunch.
- **Compression:** Optional, light-to-moderate. Use it to even the attack, not to squash the life out.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Guitar 1 pickup: bridge or bridge-plus-middle, Gain 3–5, Mids 5–7.
- Guitar 2 pickup: bridge or neck-plus-middle, Gain 2–4, Treble 6–7.
- Amp EQ: Bass 3–5, Mids 5–7, Treble 5–7.
- Reverb: 0–2.
- Pick attack: tight downstrokes for chords, alternate picking for lead figures.

If you are playing alone, do not add huge gain to fill the space. Record one part, then play the second against it. The sound is in the interlock.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Reptilia” is built around **B minor-centered** riffing with two guitars sharing jobs: one part provides clipped harmonic pressure, the other cuts through with a narrow lead figure. The parts are simple on paper, but they need exact placement.

The main vocabulary is **minor pentatonic and natural minor** with a sharp, almost new-wave attack. There is less bluesy bending than you might expect from a rock song. The notes are stated, repeated, and lined up.

The rhythm guitar teaches **staccato chord work**. Notes do not simply ring until the next change; they are stopped so the drums can speak. The lead guitar teaches **motif discipline**: repeat a small idea until it becomes the hook.

The form is direct, but the arrangement is clever. Every section feels like a gear change because the two guitars decide when to lock together and when to separate.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments below — interlocking Strokes-style parts for study.

**1. Narrow lead motif**

```
Feel: clipped eighths, ~158 BPM.

e|-----------------------------|
B|--7--7--7--10--7-------------|
G|------------------9--7-------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
   repeat until it becomes a hook
```

*Caption: Keep the line narrow and dry. Too much sustain makes it feel less like The Strokes.*

**2. Compressed chord chop**

```
Feel: short, even downstrokes.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|

D|--4--x--4--x--7--x--6--------|
A|--2--x--2--x--5--x--4--------|
E|-----------------------------|
   B5       D5     C#5
```

*Caption: The mute is part of the part. Think small amp, close mic, no extra room.*

```

E|-----------------------------|

```

*Caption: One guitar states the frame, the other pokes through the gaps. Neither part should dominate.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — interlock, compression, and clean stops.

**Drill 1 — Staccato Chord Grid**

```
~132 BPM. Downstroke, mute, repeat.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--4-x-4-x-6-x-7-x-----------|
D|--4-x-4-x-6-x-7-x-----------|
A|--2-x-2-x-4-x-5-x-----------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: clipped rhythm control. Tone: low-to-moderate gain, light compression, reverb almost off.*

**Drill 2 — Lead Motif Economy**

```
~148 BPM. Repeat without getting louder.

e|-----------------------------|
B|--7--9--10--9--7-------------|
G|-----------------9--7--------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: a tight lead hook that stays rhythmically steady. Avoid wide vibrato; this style wants straight edges.*

**Drill 3 — Two-Part Placement**

```
~120 BPM. Play Gtr 2 only in the spaces.

Gtr 1:
e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--4-----x-----4-----x--------|
D|--4-----x-----4-----x--------|
A|--2-----x-----2-----x--------|
E|-----------------------------|

Gtr 2:
e|-----------------------------|
B|-----7-----9-----10----9-----|
G|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: arrangement discipline. Record one part if you are practicing alone, then place the second part around it.*

## Make It Yours

The Strokes lesson is that two modest guitar sounds can feel bigger than one oversized one. Write a short chord chop, then write a two- or three-note lead phrase that answers it. Keep both tones dry, compressed, and narrow. If the parts step on each other, remove notes before adding effects.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **dry interlocking guitars** entering without a wide stereo wash.
> - Around 0:28, the lead figure cutting through because it is narrow and repeated.
> - Around 0:55, the rhythm guitar using mutes as part of the groove.
> - Around 1:45, the two guitars tightening instead of getting heavier.
> - Around 2:55, the final passes keeping the same clipped attitude.
