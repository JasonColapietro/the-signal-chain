# Lesson 66 — “Seven Nation Army” · The White Stripes

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** The White Stripes (Jack White)
- **Song:** “Seven Nation Army”
- **Album:** *Elephant* (2003)
- **Section:** THE 2000s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A pawnshop guitar through an octave-down pedal, making one simple riff feel like a bass, a guitar, and a warning light at once.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Key & tuning:** E minor; standard tuning, E A D G B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Jack White's sound here is famous because it refuses to behave like the clean, separated studio recipe people expected. The low hook is widely understood as guitar processed to impersonate a bass, and that is the whole lesson.

- **Guitar:** A **1960s Airline/Supro-style fiberglass guitar** is the instrument most associated with White's early White Stripes identity. The exact guitar is less important than the behavior: bright, raw pickups into a rude front end.
- **Octave:** A **DigiTech Whammy-style octave-down pedal** is commonly cited for the main hook, set to make the guitar speak one octave lower. That is why the riff feels like bass even though it comes from a guitar.
- **Fuzz/drive:** A **Big Muff-style fuzz** and old Valco/Silvertone-style amp drive are often associated with White's broader tone. On this track, keep the fuzz controlled enough that the octave line stays readable.
- **Amp:** Small vintage combo behavior is the target: mid-forward, a little ragged, not hi-fi.
- **Mic/room:** Exact recording details are not necessary for the lesson and should not be turned into fake certainty. The sound is close, dry, and deliberately rough.

This is beginner-friendly because the notes are simple. It is not beginner-proof, because the timing and attitude still matter.

## The Tone Recipe

You can build this with one pitch pedal and one dirty sound.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any guitar works. A bridge pickup with a bright voice helps the octave pedal track.
- **Octave:** Any pitch shifter with an octave-down setting. Set the dry signal low or off if you want the bass-impersonation effect.
- **Fuzz/drive:** Big Muff-style fuzz, garage overdrive, or an amp pushed into ragged breakup.
- **Amp:** A small combo, lo-fi amp model, or anything with mids and a little hair.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge or middle position, whichever tracks most clearly.
- Octave: one octave down, wet signal dominant, dry signal low-to-moderate.
- Fuzz/drive: Gain 4–7, Tone 4–6, Level matched to the band.
- Amp: Bass 4–5, Mids 6–8, Treble 4–6, Reverb 0–2.
- Pick attack: firm downstrokes. Keep every note the same length.

If the pitch pedal glitches too much, pick closer to the bridge and mute unused strings. Octave pedals reward clean input.

## What's Going On Musically

"Seven Nation Army" is built from a **minor-key riff** so simple that it became a stadium chant. The notes outline E minor territory, and the phrase is memorable because the rhythm leaves just enough space after each move.

The riff also teaches **single-string authority**. Beginners often think big tone comes from big chords, but this song proves the opposite. One string, one rhythm, and one effect can carry the entire identity of a track.

The guitar's octave-down treatment changes the arrangement. It makes the riff sit in the bass register while still keeping the pick attack of a guitar. That attack is why the line cuts through even when it sounds low.

The rest of the song is built around contrast: low hook, dry drums, then guitar layers that enter without polishing away the roughness.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments below — the famous hook is shown only as a brief fair-use excerpt.

**1. The octave-down hook**

```
Feel: steady, heavy, ~124 BPM. One octave down.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|--7--7--10--7--5--3--2-------|
E|-----------------------------|
   E  E  G   E  D  C  B
```

*Caption: Let the octave pedal make it huge. Your job is to keep the input clean and the rhythm unshaken.*

**2. Garage-fuzz power answer**

```
Feel: short and square.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--9--9--12--9--7--5--4-------|
D|--9--9--12--9--7--5--4-------|
A|--7--7--10--7--5--3--2-------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Caption: Same contour, moved into power-chord territory. Add fuzz, but keep the chords clipped.*

**3. Single-string muting**

```
Feel: all downstrokes, no open-string noise.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|--7-x-7-x-10-x-7-x-5---------|
E|-----------------------------|
   >   >    >   >   >
```

*Caption: The riff only works if the empty space stays empty.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — simple notes, serious time.

**Drill 1 — Octave Pedal Tracking**

```
~104 BPM. Pick evenly and mute unused strings.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|--5--5--8--5--3--2--0--------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: feeding a pitch shifter cleanly. Tone: octave-down dominant, fuzz moderate, no extra reverb.*

**Drill 2 — One-String Hook Builder**

```
~116 BPM. Keep every note the same length.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|--2--2--5--2--7--5--3--------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: writing memorable riffs on one string. Say the rhythm out loud before adding gain.*

**Drill 3 — Fuzz Chord Conversion**

```
~124 BPM. Convert the single-note line into clipped power chords.

e|-----------------------------|
B|-----------------------------|
G|--4--4--7--4--9--7--5--------|
D|--4--4--7--4--9--7--5--------|
A|--2--2--5--2--7--5--3--------|
E|-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: moving from hook to chorus weight without changing the rhythmic identity.*

## Make It Yours

The reusable idea is not the exact riff. It is the decision to make one guitar cover a role the band does not physically have. Try writing a hook on one string, then run it through octave-down pitch shift. If the line still works with no chords, you have something strong. Add fuzz only after the rhythm stands up on its own.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **octave-down guitar** behaving like a bass but keeping pick attack.
> - Around 0:20, the riff's rests staying clean and confident.
> - Around 0:55, the added guitar weight keeping the same blunt shape.
> - Around 2:00, the rough amp/fuzz character staying part of the charm.
> - Around 3:10, the final hook still working because the part never needed many notes.
