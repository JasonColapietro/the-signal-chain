# Lesson 103 — "Cute Without the 'E' (Cut from the Team)" · Taking Back Sunday

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Taking Back Sunday
- **Song:** "Cute Without the 'E' (Cut from the Team)"
- **Album:** *Tell All Your Friends* (2002)
- **The tone in one sentence:** Two mid-gain guitars arguing and completing each other — one holding the floor with power chords, the other throwing octave hooks and nervous high-register answers.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** Commonly learned in standard or slightly altered live/tab versions; this lesson is written in standard tuning, E A D G B E, to teach the two-guitar vocabulary.
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Early-2000s Long Island emo was not a museum of pristine studio documentation. The broad sound is clear, though: humbucker guitars, British-leaning gain, and two parts arranged so neither guitar has to be huge by itself.

- **Guitars:** Taking Back Sunday guitar rigs from this era are commonly associated with **humbucker-equipped solidbodies** — Les Paul, SG, or similar shapes show up often in the vocabulary. The exact session guitars are not the point; use something that holds power chords together and does not get ice-picky on octaves.
- **Amps:** The gain character is in the **Marshall / Mesa / high-headroom rock amp** family, depending on the player and era. Treat exact models as approximate unless you are looking at a specific live rig photo. You want crunch, not modern metal saturation.
- **Pedals:** If pedals were involved, they are not the headline. A drive or boost into an already-crunching amp is a reasonable substitute. The arrangement creates the width more than any stereo trick.
- **Tracking:** The record sound is layered and tight. Two guitars playing different jobs is the core signal chain.

The temptation is to dime the gain because the song feels frantic. Resist that. Too much distortion turns the octave lines into fog.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** crunchy humbucker guitars with enough midrange to cut through a dense band, likely doubled or layered. The studio details are not reliably documented at the knob level, so dial for behavior: articulate, urgent, and slightly raw.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker guitar. A P-90 guitar also works if the amp has enough body.
- **Amp/model:** British crunch, JCM-style, Mesa crunch, or a mid-gain amp model.
- **Drive:** Optional overdrive with gain low and level up if your amp needs a push.
- **EQ:** Mids present, bass controlled, treble clear but not sharp.

**Starting ranges (out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **5-7**.
- Bass: **4-5**, mids: **6-7**, treble: **5-6**.
- Presence: **4-6**, depending on the guitar.
- Overdrive, if used: drive **1-3**, level **5-7**.
- Pick attack: hard but not sloppy; palm mute just enough that the downstrokes stay pointed.

If you are playing both parts alone, keep the rhythm guitar a little darker and the lead/octave guitar a little brighter. That is the simplest way to fake the conversation.

## What's Going On Musically

This song is a lesson in **dual-guitar arrangement**. One guitar can play the chord roots and keep the song moving, while the other uses octaves, slides, and high-string figures to give the part its identity.

The harmony sits in a minor-leaning pop-punk world: power chords, suspended tension, and melodies that often imply the key more than the chords spell it out. Because power chords have no third, the vocal melody and the second guitar decide whether the moment feels major, minor, angry, or pleading.

The most important musical device is **interlock**. Instead of both guitars strumming the same thing, they split the job:

- Low guitar: palm-muted roots and full power-chord hits.
- High guitar: octaves that move like a second vocal line.
- Both guitars: accents landing together at phrase endings so the band snaps into focus.

That is the emo guitar lesson hiding in plain sight. The drama comes from overlap, not from one oversized part.

## The Drills

These are original exercises for the two-guitar language.

**Drill 1 — Octave Cleanliness.** Trains the fretting-hand mute that makes octave hooks usable under gain.

```
BPM target: 150, then 174. Bridge pickup, mid-gain.

e|------------------------------------------|
B|------------------------------------------|
G|--8--8--9--9--11--11--9--9--8--8---------|
D|--x--x--x--x---x---x--x--x--x--x---------|
A|--6--6--7--7---9---9--7--7--6--6---------|
E|------------------------------------------|
```

*Builds: clean octave movement. If the muted string rings as a pitch, fix the left hand before adding speed.*

**Drill 2 — Chug and Release.** Trains palm-muted verse energy into an open chorus hit.

```
BPM target: 168. Keep the hand moving in eighths.

e|------------------------------------|
B|------------------------------------|
G|--------------------6--6--4--4------|
D|--2--2--2--2--2--2--6--6--4--4------|
A|--2--2--2--2--2--2--4--4--2--2------|
E|--0--0--0--0--0--0------------------|
   PM PM PM PM PM PM  open open
```

*Builds: controlled tension. The open chords should feel like a door opening, not like a volume mistake.*

**Drill 3 — Two-Part Handoff.** Trains switching between the low role and the high role when you are the only guitarist.

```
BPM target: 156. Bar 1 rhythm, bar 2 answer.

e|------------------|--------------------------|
B|------------------|--------------------------|
G|------------------|--9--9--11--11--13--11----|
D|--2--2--6--6--4--4-|--x--x---x---x---x---x----|
A|--2--2--6--6--4--4-|--7--7---9---9--11---9----|
E|--0--0--4--4--2--2-|--------------------------|
   PM PM PM PM PM PM
```

*Builds: arrangement thinking. Make the rhythm bar darker and the octave bar brighter, even if you never touch the amp.*

## Make It Yours

When you write two guitar parts, do not double by reflex. Give each player a job. One can hold roots and mutes; the other can answer with octaves or small melodic hooks. If you are recording alone, track the low part with a little less treble and the high part with a little less gain. The result will sound wider than two identical tracks. That is the useful inheritance from this era of emo guitar: emotional density through arrangement, not through extra distortion.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00-0:20:** the guitars entering as separate voices rather than one blended wall.
> - **Verse sections:** palm-muted low parts keeping the urgency under the vocal.
> - **Pre-chorus lifts:** open accents arriving after tight muting.
> - **Chorus sections:** octave and chord parts stacking into one emotional shape.
> - **Final chorus:** how the parts feel more frantic without the gain becoming unreadable.
