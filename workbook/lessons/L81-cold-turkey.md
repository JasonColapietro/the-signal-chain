# Lesson 81 — “Cold Turkey” · John Lennon

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** John Lennon
- **Song:** “Cold Turkey”
- **Album:** Single, 1969
- **The tone in one sentence:** Two guitars moving like one damaged machine — thin, nasty, midrange-forward fuzz and overdrive, with Lennon and Eric Clapton locked into a hard unison line.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** A minor / A blues center; standard tuning (E A D G B E)
- **Section:** JOHNNY’S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

This is one of those records where the exact session chain is less important than the behavior. The guitar sound is narrow, dry, aggressive, and almost impatient. It does not feel like a polished late-'60s lead tone. It feels like a riff being dragged across the floor.

- **Guitars:** Lennon in this period is most commonly associated with his stripped-down **Epiphone Casino**, and Clapton is the other guitar voice most associated with this track. Clapton’s exact guitar for the session is not something I would state with certainty; late-'60s Clapton lore points toward Gibson humbuckers and loud amps, but the record itself matters more than the brand name.
- **Gain:** The sound is commonly described as fuzzed or heavily overdriven rather than smooth distortion. Think a fuzz or cranked amp with the low end cut tight, not a big sustaining wall.
- **Amp:** A small-to-medium tube amp pushed hard gets closer than a modern high-gain stack. The reliable clue is the texture: hard attack, little polish, not much room.
- **Studio detail:** The mic and exact amp settings are not reliably documented at a useful level, so I will not invent them. The safe description is two gritty guitar tracks, close and dry, with little sweetening.

The seed of the lesson is the unison. Lennon and Clapton are not taking turns being fancy. They are making one ugly line sound larger by doubling it with two different hands.

## The Tone Recipe

**The record path, cautiously stated:** Casino-style hollow/semi-hollow bite from Lennon, another louder electric voice from Clapton, both pushed into raw amp breakup or fuzz-like clipping, with almost no decorative ambience.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any P-90, bright humbucker, Tele bridge pickup, or single-coil guitar can work. Use the bridge pickup unless it gets painfully thin, then move to bridge-plus-neck or roll the tone down slightly.
- **Gain:** A Fuzz Face-style fuzz, a Tone Bender-style fuzz, a RAT, or a cheap overdrive into a slightly dirty amp. Set it so single notes rasp and chords almost fall apart.
- **Amp:** Fender-, Vox-, or Marshall-style clean channel pushed to edge-of-breakup. Keep the bass lower than you think.
- **Reverb/delay:** Very little. This part needs to feel trapped in the room with you.

**Starting points (all approximate ranges):**

- Gain/fuzz: **6-8**, with enough bite that palm-muted notes spit.
- Amp EQ: Bass **3-5**, mids **6-8**, treble **5-7**.
- Guitar tone: **6-8**, depending on pickup brightness.
- Pick attack: hard downstrokes and clipped releases. Do not smooth it out.
- Gain placement: put the dirt before a mostly clean amp, or run the amp just past clean and let the pedal add the ragged edge.

## What’s Going On Musically

The song sits around **A minor / A blues**. The riff language is simple on paper: minor pentatonic, the flat fifth for poison, and repeated low-root pressure. What makes it work is not harmonic complexity. It is insistence.

An **A blues scale** is A-C-D-Eb-E-G. The Eb is the flat fifth, the unstable note between the fourth and fifth. In a cleaner blues it can sound vocal. Here it sounds sick, which is exactly the point. The line does not decorate the chord; it hammers the mood into place.

The rhythm is just as important. The guitar part is short, clipped, and almost mechanical. Leave less sustain than you normally would. When two players double a riff, any looseness becomes part of the tone, so the goal is not robotic perfection. The goal is two human attacks making one rough edge.

The arrangement is also a lesson in restraint. There is very little harmonic escape. The guitar keeps returning to the same center, and the vocal sits over that pressure. If Lesson 14 teaches fuzz as color, this one teaches fuzz as discomfort.

## Signature Moves

These are short study figures in the spirit of the record, not full transcriptions.

**1. The doubled A-blues grind**

```
Feel: tense straight eighths, ~104 BPM. Keep the notes short.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|----------------------------|
D|--------5-----5b6r5---------|
A|--7--7-----7--------7--5----|
E|-------------------------8--|
   A pulse   b5 rub     fall
```

*Caption: The flat-five bend is the sour note. If two guitars play it together, the slight difference in attack makes it nastier.*

**2. The clipped unison stab**

```
Feel: hard downstrokes, stop the strings with both hands.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|----------------------------|
D|--7--7--x--7--5-------------|
A|--7--7--x--7--5--7----------|
E|--5--5--x--5--3--5----------|
   stab mute stab fall
```

*Caption: The mute is part of the phrase. Do not let the chord ring like classic rock; choke it off.*

**3. The answer bend**

```
Feel: behind the beat, ugly vibrato.

e|----------------------------|
B|----------------------------|
G|--5b7~---5------------------|
D|-----------7--5-------------|
A|----------------7~----------|
E|----------------------------|
   cry     answer  home
```

*Caption: Bend like you are pulling the note out of tune on purpose, then resolve simply.*

## The Drills

**Drill A — Unison Riff Discipline**

Trains two-guitar tightness, or one player double-tracking the same part. Record yourself once, then play the same line again without correcting the first take.

```
~104 BPM. Downstrokes only for the first pass, mixed picking for the double.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|------------------------------|
D|--------5-----------5---------|
A|--7--7-----7--5--7-----7--5---|
E|------------------------------|
   tight, clipped, no extra ring
```

*Builds: timing, muting, and the rough chorus effect that happens when two hands hit the same line.*

**Drill B — Flat-Five Pressure**

Builds comfort with the blues-scale note that makes this tone feel unwell.

```
~92 BPM. Let the Eb rub, then resolve it.

e|-------------------------------|
B|-------------------------------|
G|-------------------------------|
D|------5--6--5------------------|
A|--7------------7--5------------|
E|--------------------8--5~------|
   4  b5  4   homeward
```

*Builds: hearing the b5 as tension, not a wrong note. Tone should be dry enough that the half-step speaks clearly.*

**Drill C — Choke and Release**

Trains the right-hand stop that keeps the riff from turning into a jam.

```
~108 BPM. Hit, choke, hit, breathe.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|------------------------------|
D|--7--x--7-----5--x--5---------|
A|--7--x--7-----5--x--5--7~-----|
E|--5--x--5-----3--x--3--5~-----|
   hit stop     fall stop home
```

*Builds: aggressive muting with both hands. The silence should feel as intentional as the chord.*

## Make It Yours

The portable idea here is **doubling a simple riff until it becomes a texture**. Write a two-bar A-blues line, keep it short, then record it twice with slightly different gain or pickup choices. Pan the takes a little apart and resist the urge to clean up every rough edge. That is how a small idea becomes a record-sized part without adding notes.

Also steal the emotional use of the flat five. In a polished blues, that note can wink. In a dry, clipped fuzz tone, it can unsettle the whole track. The same scale degree changes character depending on touch, gain, and space.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:00:** the riff arriving dry and close, with very little room around it.
> - **Around 0:20:** the doubled-guitar feel — two attacks making one rough line.
> - **Around 0:45:** the flat-five tension in the blues vocabulary.
> - **Around 1:30:** clipped releases that keep the guitar from blooming into a smoother rock tone.
> - **Throughout:** the way the tone stays uncomfortable instead of getting prettier.
