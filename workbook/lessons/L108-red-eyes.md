# Lesson 108 — "Red Eyes" · The War on Drugs

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** The War on Drugs (Adam Granduciel)
- **Song:** "Red Eyes"
- **Album:** *Lost in the Dream* (2014)
- **The tone in one sentence:** A blurred heartland lead tone swimming in delay and reverb, where the guitar feels half like a lead instrument and half like weather.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** D/A-major orbit for this lesson; standard tuning, E A D G B E.
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Adam Granduciel's sound is heavily associated with layered guitars, pedals, and studio atmosphere, but "exact rig" talk gets slippery fast. The War on Drugs records often sound like several eras of guitar recording stacked together: Fender-like chime, tape-style delay, synth pads, and overdriven leads that bloom rather than bark.

- **Guitars:** Granduciel is commonly associated with **Fender offset and Tele/Strat-style guitars**, along with other studio instruments. Treat the exact guitar on a given overdub as uncertain; use a clear single-coil or bright humbucker sound.
- **Amps:** A **Fender-style clean or edge-of-breakup platform** is the most useful starting point. The amp should stay open when delay and reverb pile up.
- **Delay:** Tape-style or analog-style delay is central to this vocabulary. It should smear the note edges without turning the part into rhythmic clutter.
- **Reverb:** Plate, hall, or spring-like ambience, moderate but not endless.
- **Drive:** Low-to-medium overdrive for sustain. The lead tone has hair, but it is not a hard-rock gain sound.

The signal chain is less about one heroic pedal and more about a controlled wash: gain, delay, and reverb all just below the point where the line disappears.

## The Tone Recipe

**Record-side target:** layered clean-to-edge guitars, delay wash, and vocal lead phrases. Exact pedals and amp settings are not reliably public enough to quote as fact, so dial for feel: wide, slightly grainy, and still articulate.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Strat, Tele, Jazzmaster, or a humbucker guitar with the tone opened up.
- **Amp/model:** Fender clean, Princeton/Deluxe-style, or any clean platform with a little compression.
- **Overdrive:** Transparent or low-gain drive, gain below halfway.
- **Delay:** Tape or analog mode, low-to-medium mix, repeats fading behind the phrase.
- **Reverb:** Plate or hall, medium decay, controlled low end.

**Starting ranges (out of 10):**

- Amp gain: **3-4**, bass **4-5**, mids **5-6**, treble **5-7**.
- Overdrive gain: **2-4**, tone **4-6**, level to taste.
- Delay mix: **3-5**, feedback **3-5**.
- Reverb: **3-5**.
- Pick attack: medium-soft. Let the delay carry the tail instead of picking every gap.

If the repeats step on the next phrase, lower feedback before lowering the mix. You want atmosphere, not traffic.

## What's Going On Musically

"Red Eyes" is heartland rock through a dream machine. The guitar language is built from **major pentatonic** shapes, open-string drones, and sustained bends that feel vocal without being blues-club formal.

The harmony sits in a bright major orbit, with the guitars often implying more movement than the chord chart alone suggests. A simple lead note can feel huge because the delay repeats it into the next chord. That is the theory of the sound: **the effect extends the phrase into the harmony after it.**

Three musical moves matter most:

- **Pedal tones:** a note that keeps ringing while the chords change underneath.
- **Wide intervals:** sixths and octaves that sound open, not cramped.
- **Delayed phrasing:** leaving enough space that repeats become part of the line.

This is not shred guitar. It is landscape guitar. The hard part is trusting one bent note to do the work.

## The Drills

These are original exercises for delay-wash lead control.

**Drill 1 — Bend, Wait, Answer.** Trains leaving space for delay repeats.

```
BPM target: 96. Delay on, feedback moderate.

e|------------------------------------------|
B|--10b12~----------8--10--8---------------|
G|--------------------------9~-------------|
D|------------------------------------------|
A|------------------------------------------|
E|------------------------------------------|
   bend, let repeats speak   answer
```

*Builds: patience. Do not fill the rest after the bend; count it and let the pedal work.*

**Drill 2 — Open-String Pad Picking.** Trains ringing clean texture under a lead tone.

```
BPM target: 128. Keep every note soft.

e|--0-----0-----0-----0-----0-----0---------|
B|----3-----3-----2-----2-----0-----0-------|
G|------2-----2-----2-----2-----2-----2-----|
D|------------------------------------------|
A|------------------------------------------|
E|------------------------------------------|
```

*Builds: pad-like picking. The open string should glue the shapes together, not poke out.*

**Drill 3 — Double-Stop Horizon.** Trains wide melodic shapes with a controlled wash.

```
BPM target: 112. Medium drive, delay low.

e|--10--9--7-----7--9--10--9--7------------|
B|--10--10-8-----8--10-10--10-8------------|
G|------------------------------------------|
D|------------------------------------------|
A|------------------------------------------|
E|------------------------------------------|
```

*Builds: melodic width. Let both strings ring evenly and keep the vibrato slow.*

## Make It Yours

Set up a clean amp, one low-gain drive, one delay, and one reverb. Then play fewer notes than you normally would. Let a bend hang until the repeats decay, and answer it with a double-stop instead of a run. If the sound feels empty, add a droning open string before you add more gain. The useful lesson from "Red Eyes" is that a guitar part can be a horizon line: simple, wide, and always pulling the song forward.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **0:00-0:30:** the guitars blending with keys and ambience rather than sitting dry on top.
> - **Verse sections:** small lead phrases leaving room for delay repeats.
> - **Chorus lift:** the lead tone blooming wider without becoming hard rock.
> - **Instrumental breaks:** pentatonic bends and double-stops carrying the emotional peak.
> - **Throughout:** delay and reverb staying clear enough that the original pick attack remains audible.
