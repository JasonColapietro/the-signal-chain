# Lesson 65 — “No One Knows” · Queens of the Stone Age

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Queens of the Stone Age (Josh Homme)
- **Song:** “No One Knows”
- **Album:** *Songs for the Deaf* (2002)
- **Section:** THE 2000s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A dry, mid-pushed, low-tuned guitar sound built from C-standard weight and unfashionable vintage-combo attitude.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** C-centered rock in C standard tuning, C F Bb Eb G C

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Josh Homme's tone in this period is a reminder that "big rock guitar" does not have to mean the expected Marshall-stack recipe. The sound is thick, nasal, dry, and almost stubbornly odd.

- **Guitar:** Homme is commonly associated with **short-scale and semi-hollow oddball guitars**, including Ovation Ultra GP-style instruments in the broader Queens vocabulary. The exact guitar for every layer of this track is not something I would state as settled fact.
- **Amp:** **Ampeg VT-40/V-4-style combos and heads** are often cited as part of the Queens sound: loud, mid-forward, and not fashionable in the classic-rock sense. Treat the exact amp blend as approximate.
- **Tuning:** **C standard** is central. Tuning every string down two whole steps changes the string feel, the attack, and the way chords sit.
- **Effects:** The main rhythm identity is more amp, tuning, and EQ than an obvious modulation effect. Fuzz or boost may appear in layers, but the core lesson is the dry midrange.
- **Mic/room:** Specific mic choices are not reliably useful here. The mix impression is close, dry, and packed into the middle.

The important thing is that the guitar is not trying to sound expensive. It is trying to sound like itself.

## The Tone Recipe

You need low tuning, controlled lows, and a strong mid voice.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker or P-90 guitar that intonates well in C standard. Heavier strings are strongly recommended so the low C does not flap.
- **Amp:** An Ampeg-style model if you have it. Otherwise use a clean-to-crunch amp with a lot of mids and less fizzy top.
- **Pedals:** A low-gain fuzz, dirty boost, or overdrive can add hair, but keep it dry and tight.
- **EQ:** Do not scoop the mids. This tone gets its personality from the awkward middle frequencies.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge or bridge-plus-neck, depending on how sharp your guitar is.
- Amp: Gain 4–6, Bass 4–5, Mids 7–9, Treble 4–6.
- Fuzz/boost: Gain 2–5, Level 5–7, Tone in the middle range.
- Reverb: almost none for the main riff feel.
- Pick attack: firm, with short note lengths and confident muting.

If it sounds too modern, reduce gain and treble. The target is heavy but not glossy.

## What's Going On Musically

"No One Knows" lives in the world created by **C standard tuning**. Shapes that would be familiar in standard tuning now speak lower, darker, and looser. That lower register gives the riff its strut, but the rhythm is what makes it work.

The guitar part uses **syncopation** — accents that land between the expected beats. The riff keeps ducking and leaning forward, which gives the song its lopsided swagger. You cannot get that from tone alone; the gaps have to be played.

Harmonically, the vocabulary is rock-blues adjacent, but it avoids sounding like a bar-band blues because the tuning, EQ, and phrasing are stranger. The intervals are familiar: roots, fifths, flat thirds, and chromatic slides. The context makes them feel new.

The band arrangement is also important. The guitar leaves pockets for drums and bass, then clamps back down. Think of the riff as a drum part with pitches.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments below — low-tuned gestures in the song's spirit, not a full transcription.

**1. C-standard midrange stomp**

```
Tuning: C F Bb Eb G C. Feel: dry, swung, ~170 BPM.

C |-----------------------------|
G |-----------------------------|
Eb|----------------------------|
Bb|--2--2----5--2--------------|
F |--2--2----5--2----5--4------|
C |--0--0----3--0----3--2------|
    short   lean    fall
```

*Caption: Keep the notes clipped. The riff should feel like it is stepping around the beat, not sitting squarely on top of it.*

**2. Chromatic low-string slide**

```
Feel: greasy, but in time.

C |-----------------------------|
G |-----------------------------|
Eb|----------------------------|
Bb|----------------------------|
F |--5/7--7--5-----------------|
C |--3/5--5--3--0--0-----------|
             PM PM
```

*Caption: The slide gives the line its crooked smile. Do not let the low strings ring after the phrase ends.*

**3. Dry chord choke**

```
Feel: hit, release, silence.

C |-----------------------------|
G |-----------------------------|
Eb|--7--x--7--x--5-------------|
Bb|--7--x--7--x--5-------------|
F |--5--x--5--x--3-------------|
C |-----------------------------|
   >     >     >
```

*Caption: Queens-style heaviness often comes from short, dry chord hits. Make the mute part of the groove.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — C-standard timing and dry midrange.

**Drill 1 — Low-Tuned Pocket Riff**

```
~150 BPM. Short notes, no extra sustain.

C |-----------------------------|
G |-----------------------------|
Eb|----------------------------|
Bb|--2--2-----2-----5--4-------|
F |--2--2-----2-----5--4-------|
C |--0--0--0--0--0--3--2-------|
       PM       PM
```

*Builds: keeping low-tuned riffs tight. Tone: mids high, gain moderate, reverb off.*

**Drill 2 — Midrange Chord Chops**

```
~132 BPM. Mute immediately after every hit.

C |-----------------------------|
G |-----------------------------|
Eb|--7-x-7-x-5-x-4-x-----------|
Bb|--7-x-7-x-5-x-4-x-----------|
F |--5-x-5-x-3-x-2-x-----------|
C |-----------------------------|
   >   >   >   >
```

*Builds: dry chord punctuation. The silence should be as consistent as the chord attack.*

**Drill 3 — Swagger Subdivision**

```
~160 BPM. Accent the offbeats.

C |-----------------------------|
G |-----------------------------|
Eb|----------------------------|
Bb|----------------------------|
F |--2-----2--5-----4----------|
C |--0--0--0--3--0--2--0-------|
      PM       PM    PM
```

*Builds: syncopated low-register phrasing. Count slowly first, then bring it up without stiffening the wrist.*

## Make It Yours

The best thing to take from "No One Knows" is the permission to sound a little wrong on purpose. Tune down to C standard, use a guitar that does not feel too polite, push the mids, and write a riff that leaves gaps for the drums. If the tone starts sounding polished, you may be moving away from the point. This lesson is about personality in the midrange and rhythm in the spaces.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **dry low-tuned riff** speaking with mids instead of scooped fizz.
> - Around 0:20, the rhythm leaning around the beat rather than marching.
> - Around 1:05, the guitar and drums locking like one percussion instrument.
> - Around 2:00, the tone staying close and dry even as the arrangement opens up.
> - Around 3:30, the final riff passes keeping their odd, nasal character.
