# Lesson 68 — “Midnight in Harlem” · Tedeschi Trucks Band

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Tedeschi Trucks Band (Derek Trucks)
- **Song:** “Midnight in Harlem”
- **Album:** *Revelator* (2011)
- **Section:** THE 2010s
- **The tone in one sentence:** An SG in open E, slide straight into a singing tube amp, where intonation and touch replace the pedalboard.
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Key & tuning:** E major; open E slide tuning, E B E G# B E

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Derek Trucks is one of the clearest modern examples of hands-first tone. The gear matters, but the setup is intentionally direct.

- **Guitar:** A **Gibson SG** is the guitar most associated with Trucks. The humbuckers feed a warm, sustaining slide sound without needing much help.
- **Tuning:** **Open E** is commonly associated with this style: E B E G# B E. It lets the slide form full major chords straight across the strings.
- **Slide:** A glass slide, often discussed in the Duane Allman family of sounds, is the common reference point. The exact material matters less than fit, weight, and intonation control.
- **Amp:** A loud **Fender Super Reverb-style tube combo** is commonly cited around Trucks’s core tone: clean, loud, and starting to sing when pushed.
- **Pedals:** The famous lesson is almost none. Trucks is widely associated with going straight into the amp, using volume, picking hand, and slide pressure instead of gain pedals.
- **Mic/room:** Exact recording details are not the point here. The behavior is vocal sustain, clean headroom, and natural amp bloom.

This is the opposite of hiding behind gear. Every pitch is exposed.

## The Tone Recipe

The affordable version is about setup and discipline.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker guitar can work. An SG-style guitar feels close because of weight, access, and sustain, but the tuning and touch matter more.
- **Amp:** A clean tube combo or modeler with Fender-style headroom. Push it enough that loud notes bloom.
- **Slide:** Glass, ceramic, or brass can work. Choose one that fits your finger and lets you control pressure.
- **Pedals:** Start with none. If you need help, use only light reverb and maybe a clean boost.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: neck or middle position for warmth; bridge only if your amp is very dark.
- Amp: Gain/Volume 4–7 depending on headroom, Bass 4–5, Mids 5–7, Treble 4–6, Reverb 2–4.
- Guitar volume: 6–10 as a dynamic control, not just an on/off switch.
- Pick hand: fingers, or very soft pick attack if you are not a fingerstyle player.
- Slide pressure: light. Pressing too hard pulls notes sharp and rattles against the frets.

If the tone feels thin, check your intonation and muting before adding effects. Slide tone starts with the note being in tune.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Midnight in Harlem” lives in **E major**, with gospel and soul phrasing shaping the lead guitar. The harmony is not complicated on paper; the depth is in the voice-like way Trucks moves between notes.

Open E tuning gives you a full E major chord across the strings, so the slide can move horizontally like a singer moving through vowels. The risk is that everything can ring all the time. You have to mute behind the slide with the fretting hand and ahead of it with the picking hand.

The lead language uses **major pentatonic**, **mixolydian color**, and blues-inflected slides into chord tones. Instead of bending a string to pitch, the slide travels into the note. That means your ear, not the fret, is the final authority.

The most important musical concept is **vocal phrasing**. Trucks often sounds like he is taking a breath, singing a line, and then answering it. Silence is part of the melody.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments below — slide vocabulary in the song’s spirit, not a full transcription.

**1. Open-E vocal slide**

```
Tuning: E B E G# B E. Feel: slow 6/8, ~72 BPM.

E |-----------------------------|
B |--10/12~~----12\10--7~~------|
G#|-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
B |-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
   slide into pitch, then answer
```

*Caption: Aim the slide directly above the fret, then listen. The shimmer is only beautiful if the pitch is centered.*

**2. Major-chord sweep**

```
Feel: light touch, no hard strum.

E |--12~------------------------|
B |--12~------------------------|
G#|--12~------------------------|
E |--12~------------------------|
B |-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
   E chord at the 12th fret
```

*Caption: One straight slide gives you the whole chord. Mute the unused low strings so the sound stays graceful.*

**3. Call-and-response slide phrase**

```
Feel: sing the first line, whisper the answer.

E |-----------------------------|
B |--7/9~~-------9\7------------|
G#|------------------7/8~~------|
E |-----------------------------|
B |-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
   call          response
```

*Caption: The second phrase should feel like a human answer, not a scale exercise.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — intonation, muting, and vocal phrasing.

**Drill 1 — Slide Intonation Line**

```
~60 BPM. Land directly over each fret.

E |-----------------------------|
B |--5/7~~---7/9~~---9/12~~-----|
G#|-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
B |-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: accurate arrivals. Tone: straight into a clean amp, light reverb, no gain pedal.*

**Drill 2 — Open-E Chord Bloom**

```
~54 BPM. Strum softly, then mute cleanly.

E |--0~~~~----5~~~~----7~~~~----|
B |--0~~~~----5~~~~----7~~~~----|
G#|--0~~~~----5~~~~----7~~~~----|
E |--0~~~~----5~~~~----7~~~~----|
B |-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
```

*Builds: full-chord slide control without noise. Keep the slide parallel to the fretwire.*

**Drill 3 — Breath Between Phrases**

```
~66 BPM. Count the rests out loud.

E |-----------------------------|
B |--7/9~~-----------9\7--------|
G#|----------------------7/8~~--|
E |-----------------------------|
B |-----------------------------|
E |-----------------------------|
   phrase       rest      answer
```

*Builds: phrasing patience. Let the amp decay before the answer begins.*

## Make It Yours

The lasting lesson is that slide guitar has almost no tolerance for pretending. Tune carefully, turn off the extra pedals, and play one note until it is centered. Then make that note sound like a voice. Once your intonation and muting are honest, even a simple major-pentatonic phrase can feel complete.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **clean open space** around the band before the slide becomes central.
> - Around 1:20, the slide entering like a voice rather than a guitar lick.
> - Around 2:15, the sustain coming from hands, amp volume, and pitch control.
> - Around 3:30, the way phrases breathe instead of filling every bar.
> - Around 4:50, the upper-register slide notes staying warm rather than sharp.
