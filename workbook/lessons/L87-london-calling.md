# Lesson 87 — “London Calling” · The Clash

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** The Clash
- **Song:** “London Calling”
- **Album:** *London Calling* (1979)
- **The tone in one sentence:** Joe Strummer's dry Esquire-style chop and Mick Jones's thicker Les Paul snarl working together — one hand cuts the groove, the other gives it teeth.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** E minor; standard tuning (E A D G B E)
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

The lesson is two guitar roles, not one perfect sound.

- **Rhythm guitar:** Strummer is most associated with a battered **Fender Esquire/Telecaster-style** guitar. The bridge-pickup chop is the important behavior: bright, percussive, and dry.
- **Lead/thicker guitar:** Mick Jones is commonly associated with **Gibson Les Paul** guitars in this period, giving the track a fuller midrange voice against Strummer's cut.
- **Amps:** The Clash used loud practical stage and studio rigs rather than a fragile boutique chain. Fender, Music Man, Marshall, and similar late-'70s workhorse references all appear around the band, but exact track settings are not the point here.
- **Gain:** Crunch, not metal. The guitars still have string definition.
- **Effects:** Minimal. The record's force comes from hands, arrangement, bass, and drums.

This is punk as arrangement discipline. The rhythm part is not random slashing. It is a controlled, syncopated engine.

## The Tone Recipe

**The record path, cautiously stated:** bright single-coil rhythm guitar through a loud clean-to-crunch amp, paired with a thicker humbucker guitar adding midrange bite.

**Affordable path:**

- **Rhythm side:** Tele bridge pickup, Strat bridge pickup, or any bright guitar with the tone open.
- **Thicker side:** Les Paul-style bridge pickup, SG, or humbucker-equipped guitar.
- **Amp:** Clean amp with a little breakup, or low-gain overdrive.
- **Pedals:** A light overdrive or RAT-style pedal with gain low can work.

**Starting points (all approximate ranges):**

- Rhythm gain: **3-5**.
- Lead/thick gain: **4-6**.
- EQ: Bass **3-5**, mids **6-8**, treble **5-7**.
- Reverb: **0-2**.
- Pick attack: downstrokes with fast left-hand pressure releases. The mute is part of the chord.

## What's Going On Musically

The song centers on **E minor**. The main drive comes from a low E-root pressure and a dark minor-key chord movement. The bass and drums are crucial; the guitar does not need to fill every frequency.

The big concept is **syncopated chopping**. Syncopation means the accent lands off the obvious downbeat. Strummer's rhythm style often sounds wild because the hand is moving constantly while the fretting hand decides which hits speak and which hits are muted.

Use partial chords. A full six-string grip can get too big and polite. Two or three strings, hit hard and stopped quickly, give the track its urgency.

The second guitar role is contrast. A Les Paul-style snarl can hold longer notes or thicker stabs while the bright guitar keeps the motor running. If both guitars play the same frequency range the same way, the arrangement gets smaller.

## Signature Moves

These are short study figures in the track's vocabulary.

**1. Esquire chop**

```
Feel: driving, ~136 BPM. Keep the hand moving.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|--9--x--9-----7--x--7---------|
D|--9--x--9-----7--x--7---------|
A|--7--x--7-----5--x--5---------|
E|------------------------------|
   hit mute hit fall mute
```

*Caption: The dead strums make the groove. Do not remove them to make the part cleaner.*

**2. Low E pressure**

```
Feel: palm-muted, urgent eighths.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|------------------------------|
D|------------------------------|
A|------5-----7-----5-----------|
E|--0-0---0-0---0-0---0-3-------|
   PM.   PM.   PM.
```

*Caption: The low E is the alarm bell. Keep it tight so the bass can still speak.*

**3. Thick answer chord**

```
Feel: bigger guitar, let it bark then stop.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|--9-----7-----5---------------|
D|--9-----7-----5-----7---------|
A|--7-----5-----3-----7---------|
E|--------------------5---------|
   snarl  fall  answer
```

*Caption: This is the Les Paul job: fewer hits, more body.*

## The Drills

**Drill A — Constant-Hand Chop**

Trains right-hand motion with fretting-hand mutes.

```
~128 BPM. The strumming hand never stops.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|--9--x--9--x--7--x--7--x------|
D|--9--x--9--x--7--x--7--x------|
A|--7--x--7--x--5--x--5--x------|
E|------------------------------|
   down up down up, all controlled
```

*Builds: percussive rhythm. The x hits should be the same tempo as the chord hits.*

**Drill B — Bright and Thick Split**

Trains arranging two guitar parts in different registers.

```
~136 BPM. Part 1 bright stabs; Part 2 low answer.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|--9--x--9-------7--x--7-------|  part 1
D|------------------------------|
A|----------7--7----------5--5--|  part 2
E|----------0--0----------0--0--|
```

*Builds: hearing two roles at once. Record the low answer separately if you can.*

**Drill C — Punk Dynamic Stop**

Trains hard stops without losing time.

```
~132 BPM. Silence on beat 4 must stay in tempo.

e|------------------------------|
B|------------------------------|
G|--9--9--9------7--7--7--------|
D|--9--9--9------7--7--7--------|
A|--7--7--7------5--5--5--------|
E|----------(rest)-------(rest)-|
   1  &  2   4   1  &  2   4
```

*Builds: ensemble discipline. Stop with both hands and come back in without rushing.*

## Make It Yours

Write one bright, clipped rhythm part and one thicker answer part. Keep them out of each other's way. That is the lesson here: punk guitars can be arranged as carefully as any harmony stack if the roles are clear.

Also practice muting as a positive sound. A dead strum is not a mistake between chords. It is the snare drum you get for free when your right hand stays moving.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:00:** the dry guitar chop sitting against the bass figure.
> - **Around 0:30:** bright rhythm cut and thicker guitar body occupying different spaces.
> - **Around 1:00:** muted strums keeping the track moving between chord hits.
> - **Around 1:45:** crunch that stays articulate rather than turning into heavy-gain smear.
> - **Throughout:** the band feel — guitar tone as rhythm-section behavior.
