# Lesson 64 — “Chop Suey!” · System of a Down

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** System of a Down (Daron Malakian)
- **Song:** “Chop Suey!”
- **Album:** *Toxicity* (2001)
- **Section:** THE 2000s
- **The tone in one sentence:** A dry, percussive drop-C guitar sound that snaps between violent chugging and bright melodic figures.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** C-centered minor/modal rock; drop C tuning, C G C F A D

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Daron Malakian’s guitar sound on this era of System of a Down is less about polish and more about contrast. The part has to go from surgical low-string hits to almost folk-like melodic movement without feeling like a different player.

- **Guitar:** An **Ibanez Iceman** is commonly cited as one of Malakian’s most associated guitars in this era, usually feeding a bridge humbucker sound.
- **Amp:** A **Marshall-style high-gain rig** is the behavior most associated with the tone: dry, mid-present, and fast under the pick. Exact studio amp details should be treated as approximate rather than a single magic box.
- **Effects:** The main rhythm tone is not dependent on a visible wash of effects. If there is ambience in the recording, it is not the lesson’s center.
- **Gain:** Enough saturation to make the low C bark, but not so much that the stops blur.
- **Mic/room:** Specific mic placements are not reliably documented at a useful level, so the practical target is close, dry, and immediate.

This is a right-hand lesson. The rig helps, but the whiplash comes from muting, accent control, and clean stops.

## The Tone Recipe

You can build the sound with a simple high-gain setup.

**Substitutions:**

- **Guitar:** Any humbucker guitar that holds drop C tuning. Heavier strings help the low C stay stable.
- **Amp:** A Marshall, Mesa, 5150-style, or modeler high-gain patch with the lows controlled and mids present.
- **Pedals:** A low-drive overdrive can tighten the amp if needed. Avoid wide chorus or long delay on the chugs.
- **Tuning:** Drop C is part of the vocabulary. The one-finger shapes and the string tension change the feel.

**Starting points (knobs out of 10):**

- Pickup: bridge humbucker.
- Amp: Gain 5–7, Bass 4–6, Mids 5–7, Treble 5–7, Presence 4–6.
- Boost: optional, Drive 1–3, Tone 5–6, Level 5–7.
- Reverb: 0–2 for rhythm.
- Pick attack: hard and precise. The silence after a hit matters as much as the hit.

If the riff feels slow, do not add gain. Tighten the palm mute and shorten the notes.

## What’s Going On Musically

“Chop Suey!” lives in a **C-centered** drop-tuned world, with a lot of color coming from minor-mode movement and sudden dynamic shifts. The song’s identity is the jump from delicate arpeggios to clipped low-string force.

Drop C tuning gives the guitar a broad low root, but the song avoids becoming a single-note grind. The contrast comes from **register shifts**: low C chugs, then higher melodic shapes, then back again. That vertical jump is what makes the arrangement feel unstable in a useful way.

The rhythm language is built on **tight sixteenth-note control**. Even when the part feels frantic, the best way to practice it is slowly, with the muting exact. Every open string you did not intend to hear will be obvious in this tuning.

The melodic side uses simple minor shapes and open-string resonance. That is the System trick: folk-like directness next to metal attack.

## Signature Moves

Short study fragments below — characteristic gestures for study, not the full song.

**1. Drop-C stop-start chug**

```
Tuning: C G C F A D. Feel: clipped sixteenths, ~128 BPM.

D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
F|-----------------------------|
C|--0-0-x-0-0-x-3--2----------|
G|--0-0-x-0-0-x-3--2----------|
C|--0-0-x-0-0-x-3--2----------|
   PM PM   PM PM
```

*Caption: The rests have to be as sharp as the attacks. Use both hands to stop the low C.*

**2. Bright arpeggio contrast**

```
Feel: lighter hand, let notes connect.

D|--------0-----------0--------|
A|-----1-----1-----3-----3-----|
F|--2-----------0--------------|
C|-----------------------------|
G|-----------------------------|
C|-----------------------------|
   minor color, no heavy chug
```

*Caption: Same guitar, different touch. Let the top strings speak without dragging the low C along.*

**3. Whiplash switch**

```
Feel: quiet to hard in one bar.

D|--------0-----------|----------------|
A|-----1-----1--------|----------------|
F|--2-----------2-----|----------------|
C|--------------------|--0-0-0-0-3-2---|
G|--------------------|--0-0-0-0-3-2---|
C|--------------------|--0-0-0-0-3-2---|
   melodic             chug
```

*Caption: The transition is the exercise. Do not let the delicate bar rush into the heavy one.*

## The Drills

Original exercises in the style of the song — all about control in drop C.

**Drill 1 — Low-C Gate With Your Hands**

```
~104 BPM. Kill every x with both hands.

D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
F|-----------------------------|
C|--0-x-0-x-0-0-x-5--3--------|
G|--0-x-0-x-0-0-x-5--3--------|
C|--0-x-0-x-0-0-x-5--3--------|
   PM  PM  PM PM
```

*Builds: percussive muting without relying on a noise gate. Tone: dry high gain, bridge pickup, mids present.*

**Drill 2 — Melody-to-Chug Reset**

```
~92 BPM. Two beats light, two beats heavy.

D|--------0-----------|----------------|
A|-----3-----3--------|----------------|
F|--2-----------2-----|----------------|
C|--------------------|--0--0--3--2----|
G|--------------------|--0--0--3--2----|
C|--------------------|--0--0--3--2----|
```

*Builds: switching pick depth and muting position instantly. The arpeggio should not sound distorted into mush.*

**Drill 3 — Drop-C Accent Ladder**

```
~128 BPM. Accent only the marked hits.

D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
F|-----------------------------|
C|--0-0-0-0-0-0-0-0-3--2------|
G|--0-0-0-0-0-0-0-0-3--2------|
C|--0-0-0-0-0-0-0-0-3--2------|
   >     >       >     >
```

*Builds: making repeated low notes speak in phrases instead of a flat stream. Keep the wrist loose.*

## Make It Yours

The portable idea is contrast. Take any heavy drop-tuned riff and put a fragile, higher-register answer right before it. Then practice the change until the two parts feel like one sentence. In this style, tone is not just the amp setting. It is how quickly your hands can move from open resonance to total shutdown.

> **Listen For**
>
> - Around 0:00, the **bright arpeggiated opening** before the heavy guitar arrives.
> - Around 0:25, the first hard shift into **dry drop-C force**.
> - Around 0:50, the clipped low-string hits staying short and controlled.
> - Around 1:30, the melodic sections keeping their own voice instead of becoming metal chords.
> - Around 2:35, the arrangement using sudden stops as part of the hook.
