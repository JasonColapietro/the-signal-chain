# Lesson 86 — “Killer Queen” · Queen (Brian May)

## At a Glance

- **Artist:** Queen (Brian May)
- **Song:** “Killer Queen”
- **Album:** *Sheer Heart Attack* (1974)
- **The tone in one sentence:** Red Special bell tones stacked into miniature guitar choirs — bright, compressed, harmonized lines that sparkle without losing the midrange voice.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Key & tuning:** E-flat major with quick chromatic turns; standard tuning (E A D G B E)
- **Section:** JOHNNY'S FAVORITES

## The Rig & Signal Chain

Brian May's core voice is better documented than most, but the exact overdub recipe for any one layer still deserves caution.

- **Guitar:** The **Red Special** is the guitar most associated with May: homemade with his father, fitted with switching options that allow in-phase and out-of-phase pickup combinations. Those options are a big part of the bell-like and nasal colors.
- **Pick:** A coin, famously a sixpence, is the picking tool most associated with him. The hard edge gives the note a bright front.
- **Boost:** A Rangemaster-style treble booster, or a closely related boost, is commonly cited as the front-end push into the amp.
- **Amp:** Vox **AC30** combos are the amp family most associated with May's studio and live sound. Cranked EL84 compression gives the sustain and midrange focus.
- **Studio detail:** The specific pickup combination and track count for each little harmony part are not public facts I would overstate. The safe claim is layered Red Special-family tones, carefully arranged.

Read this beside Lesson 48. “Bohemian Rhapsody” shows the large guitar orchestra; “Killer Queen” shows the same discipline in smaller, sharper details.

## The Tone Recipe

**The record path, cautiously stated:** Red Special into treble boost and Vox-style amp compression, layered in short harmonized overdubs with bright attack and controlled sustain.

**Affordable path:**

- **Guitar:** Any bright electric. Single-coils help; a humbucker with tone rolled down slightly can work if the amp has enough upper mids.
- **Boost:** Treble booster, clean boost with tone lift, or low-gain overdrive with the level up.
- **Amp:** Vox AC-style model or any clean-to-crunch amp with chime.
- **Pick:** Try a coin or very hard pick to hear how much the attack changes.

**Starting points (all approximate ranges):**

- Boost: gain low-to-medium, level **6-8**.
- Amp gain: **4-6**, just compressed enough that notes bloom.
- EQ: Bass **3-5**, mids **6-8**, treble **6-8**.
- Guitar tone: **6-8**.
- Reverb: low. Let the layers make the width.

## What's Going On Musically

“Killer Queen” is harmonically quick. It moves through polished pop changes with chromatic passing chords, so the guitar part has to be arranged, not merely played over the top.

The core device is **harmonized melody**. May often treats the guitar like a small horn section: one line states the melody, another line shadows it a third or sixth away, and a third layer may complete a triad. A **triad** is a three-note chord: root, third, and fifth. When you spread triads across guitar tracks, you get a clean orchestral sound without strumming big chords.

The tone has to stay clear because the harmony is dense. Too much gain turns thirds into mush. The sweet spot is compressed and sustaining, but still articulate enough that each note in the harmony has a job.

The rhythmic feel is also tidy. These parts do not sprawl. They enter, sparkle, and get out.

## Signature Moves

These are original miniature figures in May's arranged-guitar vocabulary.

**1. Bell-like third harmony**

```
Feel: clipped and elegant, ~118 BPM.

e|--6----8----10----8----------|  upper voice
B|--8----9----11----9----------|  lower voice
G|-----------------------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
   bright parallel thirds
```

*Caption: Keep the gain low enough that both voices remain separate.*

**2. Triad fanfare**

```
Feel: short stabs, let each chord bloom.

e|--6-----8-----6--------------|
B|--8-----9-----8--------------|
G|--8-----8-----8--------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
   Eb    color  return
```

*Caption: A small triad can sound orchestral if the attack is bright and the release is clean.*

**3. Coin-edge answer**

```
Feel: light, scratched attack.

e|-----------------------------|
B|--8--6-----------------------|
G|-------8--7--5~--------------|
D|-----------------------------|
A|-----------------------------|
E|-----------------------------|
   little vocal fall
```

*Caption: Let the pick edge speak at the start of each note, then get out of the way.*

## The Drills

**Drill A — Two-Part Bell Harmony**

Trains clean parallel harmony without extra noise.

```
~104 BPM. Record one line, then double the other.

e|--6--8--10--11--10--8--6------|  upper
B|--8--9--11--13--11--9--8------|  lower
G|------------------------------|
D|------------------------------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   equal volume, equal release
```

*Builds: disciplined harmony tracking. The two lines should sound arranged, not improvised at the same time.*

**Drill B — Treble-Boost Touch**

Trains bright attack without harshness.

```
~112 BPM. Accent the first note of each group.

e|--------6--------8--------10--|
B|-----8--------9--------11-----|
G|--8--------8--------10--------|
D|------------------------------|
A|------------------------------|
E|------------------------------|
   triads picked lightly
```

*Builds: control with a bright rig. If the top string stings, soften the pick before changing every knob.*

**Drill C — Short Arrangement Cells**

Trains entering and leaving cleanly.

```
~118 BPM. Two beats of music, two beats of rest.

e|--6--8--6--------|--8--10--8-------|
B|--8--9--8--------|--9--11--9-------|
G|-----------------|-----------------|
D|-----------------|-----------------|
A|-----------------|-----------------|
E|-----------------|-----------------|
   cell 1           cell 2
```

*Builds: arrangement discipline. Stop the strings cleanly at the end of each cell.*

## Make It Yours

Write a four-note melody and harmonize it two ways: first in thirds, then as triads. Record the parts separately. That is the doorway into May's world. The magic is not just the gear; it is the patience to build a part one voice at a time.

Also notice the restraint of short guitar statements. You can make a track sound expensive without playing constantly. A bright, harmonized two-beat answer in the right spot can do more than a full bar of lead guitar.

> **Listen For**
>
> - **Around 0:15:** little guitar answers that feel arranged like vocal or horn parts.
> - **Around 0:45:** bright attack with a compressed, singing middle.
> - **Around 1:10:** harmony lines that stay clear because the gain is controlled.
> - **Around 1:45:** guitar parts entering briefly and leaving cleanly.
> - **Throughout:** the Red Special family of bell, honk, and choir colors in miniature.
